# AIBIKI — STYLE BIBLE

Expands `bible/pillars.md`; obeys `engine/chapter-engine.md` and `engine/art-spec.md`.
Nothing here may contradict pillars. This file is the craft manual: how a manzai act becomes
a printed page, how these two people are allowed to talk, and what is forbidden.

---

## 0. BUBBLE DISCIPLINE — the law

- **4-8 words per bubble. HARD CAP.** No exceptions, no "just this once," no long final line.
- **8-14 bubbles per page** (default 10-12). Splash page: 3-6.
- **Maximum 3 bubbles in any one panel.**
- **Density comes from COUNT, never from LENGTH.** If a page feels thin, add bubbles and add
  panels. Never lengthen a bubble. Six quick lines across four small panels is the texture.
- A long thought becomes a **bubble chain**: 3-6 short bubbles, same speaker, across 2-3
  panels, each one a separate breath. Never one fat bubble.
- **NOBODY EXPLAINS THEIR FEELINGS. EVER.** This cast argues about the set list, the bill, the
  quota, the pause length, whose turn it is to carry the case, and what time the train was.
  That is the entire emotional vocabulary of the series and it is enough.
- **THE REWRITE RULE (mechanical — apply it every time):** if a line states an emotion, delete
  it and rewrite it as a note about **timing** or an **object**.
  - "I'm worried about you" becomes "You've been standing since four."
  - "I'm proud of you" becomes "You held it three-quarters. Don't fix it."
  - "I don't want to lose this" becomes "The quota's ten. We've sold six."
  - "I missed you" becomes "The express ran nine seconds early."
  - If the rewrite contains no object and no number, it is not finished.
- Words the leads never say to each other across 55 chapters: *love, lonely, feelings,
  honestly, the truth is, I've always.* Not as denial, not as a tease. They do not own them.

## 1. WRITING MANZAI ON THE PAGE

The series lives or dies here. A bit is never summarized in a caption when it can be performed
in panels. If a chapter contains the sentence "they killed," that chapter is failing.

### 1.1 THE BIT UNIT — four panels, always in this order

Every joke on stage renders as a **four-panel unit**. It may compress into three panels or
stretch across five, but the order never changes:

1. **SETUP panel** — usually WIDE. Gaku speaks. Both figures inside the light pool. 1-2
   bubbles. The only panel where the reader is allowed to see the whole stage.
2. **BOKE panel** — small, tight on Komako, chest-up or closer. 1-2 bubbles. Her nonsense
   arrives at full conviction. Background: white paper or nothing.
3. **BEAT panel** — the hole. Small. **Gaku's face, no bubble**, thumb ticking, eye highlight
   gone. This panel is where the joke actually happens; the words are only scaffolding around
   it. Never skip it. Never fill it.
4. **TSUKKOMI panel** — small or big depending on the size of the laugh. 1-2 bubbles. His
   correction names the exact error in her logic. Optional SFX: PAM (palm-smack) on the
   shoulder — **onstage only, never offstage, not once in 55 chapters.**

A set is four to seven of these units. Vary which panel gets the size: a big BOKE panel means
she took the room; a big BEAT panel means he is in trouble.

### 1.2 KAKEAI AS BUBBLE-CHAINS

When they get rolling — the thing the reader came for — drop the four-panel unit and run
**kakeai**: five to nine alternating bubbles across two or three small panels, 4-8 words each,
no beat panels, no reaction shots, no room. Rules:

- Alternate strictly K-G-K-G. The moment the alternation breaks, the run is over.
- Bubbles overlap panel borders during a run. That is correct manga texture, and it is how the
  page tells the reader the speed changed.
- A run ends one of three ways: **she wins** (he stops; one bubbleless panel of him looking at
  her), **he wins** (SFX: PAM), or **the train** (see 1.6).
- Maximum one full kakeai run per page. Two runs on one page reads as noise, not as speed.

### 1.3 THE BOKE-LOGIC RULE (absolute)

**Komako's nonsense is always secretly correct — one beat late.** She is never merely random.
Every boke line is a true statement wearing the wrong clothes, and the reader should feel the
click a half-second after Gaku's retort has already moved on. That lag is the series' laugh.

- Legal: "That's not a line. That's grief." (Correct. It is a funeral queue. It is grief.)
- Legal: "Escalators are just very patient stairs." (Correct. Structurally, yes.)
- **Illegal:** randomness for its own sake; non-sequiturs with no back door onto a truth;
  "silly girl" comedy; anything whose joke is that she is stupid. She is the smartest performer
  in the book and the script may never forget it, not even inside a bit.
- The writer's test: after drafting a boke line, write the one sentence explaining why it is
  actually true. If that sentence does not exist, it is not an AIBIKI line.

### 1.4 LAUGH LETTERING — three sounds, three meanings

Crowd sound is **never** inside a speech bubble. It is hand-lettered onto the page.

- **WAHHA** — the mass. Big freehand overlapping letters across the bottom third of the panel,
  crowding the figures, sometimes clipped by the panel border. Size equals size of laugh. Draw
  it as a *weight pressing upward* out of the crowd silhouette. Two to four times per set, no
  more; a page where every panel has WAHHA has no laughs in it at all.
- **KUSU** — the single giggle. Tiny, alone, in an ocean of white, usually an upper corner.
  One person laughed. In some chapters this is worse than silence and in some it is better;
  what decides is what else is in the panel with it.
- **SHIIN** — the fatal silence. Large, thin, hollow **outlined** letters with nothing inside
  them, laid over a fully white panel with the crowd bank drawn but toneless. Silence is the
  only sound in this series that gets white lettering. Cap: **twice per chapter maximum**, and
  a chapter that opens on SHIIN may not close on it.
- **Escalation grammar:** KUSU into WAHHA is a set turning. WAHHA into SHIIN is a set dying,
  and it has to happen inside three panels or it stops being funny and becomes sad.

### 1.5 CUTTING AWAY — the two legal cuts, and when

Mid-set you may leave the stage in exactly two directions. Both are earned, never decorative.

- **CUT TO CROWD** — the black silhouette bank, white teeth-gaps, Ōgaki the one real face.
  Use it after the third bit of a set (the room has turned), on any bit-bleed beat, and never
  more than **once per page**. A cut to the crowd during a dying set draws the bank with **no
  gaps at all** — a flat black bar. That is what bombing looks like from the stage.
- **CUT TO SUGA'S GLASSES** — the booth: a black rectangle, two flat white ovals. Use it when
  the set is being **judged rather than enjoyed** — on the BEAT panel of the most important
  bit, right after a rewritten line lands, on the last bit of an audition set. The glasses
  never react. What tells the reader is the **glass and the towel**: fast circles means you
  rushed it; a long stare at the glass means you had something and dropped it; the glass set
  down unfinished means the best thing he has seen this year. Cap: **once per chapter**; twice
  only in chapters 28, 44, 54 and 55.

### 1.6 THE EXPRESS-PAUSE NOTATION

The 21:40 express passes directly over Gakeppuchi mid-first-set (locals never say the time;
they say "the express"). Veterans write the pause; amateurs get flattened. Gaku writes a
**four-second hole** into every first set.

It is notated **inside the panel description**, never as a new line type — the lint parses only
the five legal line types (speech, thought, aside, SFX, caption) and inventing a sixth breaks
the build:

    PANEL 5 [wide]: EXPRESS PAUSE — four seconds. Both hold position mid-gesture inside the
    light pool. Neither looks up. The tone across the arch vibrates: short horizontal ticks.
    - SFX: GATAN-GOTON (rattle-clack)

Rules: the pause panel carries **no speech bubbles**, so the page must budget its 8-14 total
elsewhere. Nobody ever mentions the train on stage. Offstage they discuss it constantly, as a
scheduling problem, which is the actual joke. At ch55 the hole is performed in a broadcast hall
with no railway line anywhere near it; the judges never know; the reader does.

### 1.7 THE BIT-BLEED RULE — the series' knife

**When a true feeling surges on stage, the crowd laughs HARDER, because they think it is
material.** The truest sentence either of them says in 55 chapters is received as a punch line
by sixty strangers. Rendering, in order:

1. The line is written **exactly like a bit line** — 4-8 words, in rhythm, indistinguishable on
   the page from any other bubble. No italics, no wobbly bubble, no tone, no signal of any kind.
   If the reader can tell from the lettering, the beat is dead.
2. The next panel carries the largest **WAHHA** on the page.
3. The panel after *that* is the partner's face, no bubble, held one beat too long — and that
   panel is what the whole chapter was about.
4. Optional and lethal: cut to Ōgaki, the one real face in the bank, not laughing.

**RATION: ONCE PER ARC, MAXIMUM. Locked slots: ch5, ch22, ch31, ch44, ch55.** A sixth use
anywhere in the series is a rejection, not a note. It only cuts because it is rare.

## 2. THE ADDRESS LADDER

The romance has no confession for 54 chapters, so **what they call each other IS the progress
bar.** It moves three times. Each move is a plot event and each happens **mid-chore** — hands
busy, eyes elsewhere, nothing dramatic in the room. Never during a fight, a set, a win, a loss,
or a goodbye.

### 2.1 THE LEADS

| Rung | Chapters | Komako to Gaku | Gaku to Komako | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — offstage baseline | 1-35 | "Tokita" | "Hayase" | Everywhere off the stage, all series. |
| 0s — **stage forms** | 1-54 | **"Gaku-sensei"** | **"Hayase-san"** | Onstage only, in character. |
| 1 — **THE SLIP** | **ch36** | (unchanged) | **"Komako," once** | Mid-chore (re-taping the mic cable), unremarked. |
| 2 — **THE RUNG** | **ch55** | **"Gaku"** | **"Komako"** | GRAN PORTO booth, splitting the bill. |

**The stage forms are the thesis of the series rendered as grammar.** Onstage she is *more*
familiar than she ever is offstage ("Gaku-sensei" — a mock honorific about his writing, which
the regulars know as part of the act). Onstage he is *more* formal than he ever is offstage
("Hayase-san" — the -san he drops the moment they step out of the light). They are closer
inside the bit than outside it, in the words themselves, on every single stage page.

**THE SLIP (ch36, locked).** Gaku says "Komako" exactly once, buried mid-sentence, while both
their hands are busy — re-dressing the mic cable with her 19mm cloth tape, the Marumo tape,
the one place her two lives touch on purpose — inside a sentence about a bracket deadline. Neither stops. Neither looks up. It is never mentioned again by anyone, ever,
including the narration. It does not recur. The reader will remember it for nineteen chapters,
which is the point. Rule: the panel it lands in must be **small**, and the page must not treat
it as important in any way.

**THE KNIFE (structural, use it constantly).** Everyone else in their lives uses their given
names. Mizobe and Suehiro have called them "Komako" and "Gaku" for nine years. Her family says
"Koma-chan." Ōgaki says "sensei." The reader is surrounded by these two people's given names,
spoken warmly, by everybody — except the one person who should say them. Put a cohort scene
near a two-hander whenever the chapter needs to hurt without an incident.

### 2.2 THE ENSEMBLE

| Speaker | Form | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone to Shizue | **"Shachō"** | Nobody in 55 chapters uses her name to her face. |
| Everyone to Suga | **"Master"** | Including Shizue, in the two lines she has about him. |
| Shizue to the leads | "Hayase." "Tokita." | Surname, no honorific, full stop. Contractual. |
| Suga to Komako | "Hayase" | The only one of the two he has ever named. |
| Suga to Gaku | **"the pen"** | He has never used Gaku's name. Not once. Reserve it. |
| Kujirai to the leads | "Hayase-san" / "Tokita-san" | And **"AIBIKI-san"** to the unit, addressing a two-person act as one person. Running joke; nobody corrects her. |
| The leads to Kujirai | "Kujirai-san" | Always. Even at ch41. |
| Ōgaki to the leads | **"Sensei"** — both | Mortifies Gaku. Delights Komako. Never corrected. |
| The leads to Higuchi | "onēsan" (ch1-32) → **"Higuchi-san"** (ch33+) | They learn her name from a new name tag the same chapter she speaks her first full sentence. |
| Konuma to Gaku | "Tokita" | Peer to peer. The only person who says it as an equal. |
| Nukui to Komako | **"Hayase"**, no honorific | Same form Shizue uses, for a completely different reason. |
| Mizobe / Suehiro to the leads | **"Komako" / "Gaku"** | Given names, nine years, warm. See THE KNIFE. |

### 2.3 THE GEKKŌ FORMS — reserved for the thaw

What they called each other on stage and at home, 1987-2003:

- **Shizue to Suga: "Tetsu-san."**
- **Suga to Shizue: "Shizu."**

Neither form has been spoken aloud since 2003. **CH54 IS THE NAME CHAPTER (locked cluster):**
Suga says "Shizu" out loud for the first time in 23 years, and — in the same chapter, handing
over the mic case — says "**Tokita**" for the first time ever. Two names return in one chapter,
one rung ahead of the leads, who get theirs at ch55. Neither elder explains anything. Nobody in
the room reacts out loud; Komako's palms go flat on the table, which is the only acknowledgment
the scene is permitted.

**RULE, absolute:** every rung change on this ladder happens with someone's hands busy —
taping, sweeping, folding flyers, splitting a bill, watering a pavement. If a name lands during
a speech, a confession, a hug, a rain scene, or a station platform, the scene is rewritten.

## 3. HOW THE INDUSTRY SPEAKS

The opponent is weather, not a villain. Machine-speech is therefore never sinister, never
stupid, and never wrong — it is simply built out of a different material than a laugh. It is
allowed to be funny. It is never allowed to be a straw man.

### 3.1 KUJIRAI'S KPI DIALECT (invented, deadpan-plausible, recurring)

Use these as real vocabulary. Recur them. Never define them in dialogue — the reader picks
them up the way the leads had to.

| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| **clip-through** | Percentage of a vertical clip watched past the setup. Four percent is alive. |
| **the fourteen** | The first fourteen seconds. If the boke has not broken by then, the clip is dead. |
| **vertical is a door, not a betrayal** | Kujirai's entire thesis, said at least once per arc. |
| **room conversion** | Percentage of a live audience that follows the account afterward. |
| **warm seat / cold seat** | A returning ticket-buyer counts half; a first-timer counts double. |
| **quota bleed** | How much of the ten-ticket NORMA the act eats itself. Money, not metaphor. |
| **second-screen safe** | A bit that survives being half-watched. Said as praise. It is not praise. |
| **the stack** | A night's running order, as a noun. "You're third in the stack." |
| **trim to forty** | Ninety televised seconds cut to forty. Happens at ch17. |
| **the shelf** | Where an act goes when a network likes them and has no slot. |
| **the arrow** | An act's trajectory on her sheet: up, flat, down. Never shown to the act. |
| **panel-ready** | A performer bookable for talk panels **without their partner.** The most dangerous word in the series. It is spoken about Komako, in front of Gaku, at ch41, by someone being kind. |

### 3.2 SCOUT POLITENESS

Soft, complimentary, passive-voiced, and always with an exit pre-built into the sentence:
"we're only having conversations at this stage," "nothing is on paper yet," "if the timing were
different." The scout never lies and is never rude; **he is right about her and that is the
whole problem.** His tell is grammatical: he says **"we"** for the network and **"you"
singular** when he means one of them — and he does it while both of them are standing there.
Nobody catches it in dialogue. The reader catches it. Cap: he appears in ch11, ch41, and once
more at the showrunner's discretion; he never becomes an antagonist and never appears at ch55.

### 3.3 TABLOID HEADLINE GRAMMAR

Tabloid text is **never in a speech bubble and never read aloud by a character.** It is drawn
as a **hard-geometric black-bordered box** laid across the panel at a slight angle, all caps,
particles stripped out, with a small-print subhead in a lighter weight. The question mark does
the accusing. It always overlaps something ordinary: a bowl, a hand, a ticket stub.

- `AIBIKI — OFFSTAGE TOO?`
- `MOVER GIRL, 24: WHO WRITES HER?`
- `ROOM 302: NIGHT DESK, TWO COFFEES`

Rule: the leads never correct a tabloid, never hold a press conference, and never explain
themselves to anyone. External misreads are legal (pillars); between the two of them there is
only the unsaid, never the mistaken. The tabloid is weather. You do not argue with weather.

### 3.4 AWARD-SHOW MC PATTER

Three-beat cadence, sponsor named every time, present tense, no adjectives about the acts:
"Nine acts. One stage. One title." / "Brought to you by KOGANE FOODS." / "Entry twelve —
please, the stage is yours." MC patter is always **loud, bright, and slightly too fast**, and
it is always immediately followed by a panel of something silent: a hand on a curtain, a
number badge, a strip of tape on a mic stand.

### 3.5 THE CUT RULE (absolute)

**Machine-speech is always cut against a real object.** Every page of industry dialogue must
put a physical, priced, touchable thing in frame — a ¥299 drink-bar receipt, the sweating brick
wall, a plastic tray, the daruma, a ten-ticket NORMA stub, a lift gate. Never two consecutive
panels of people saying numbers to each other with nothing in their hands. The object is the
counterweight; without it the industry reads as a villain, and it is not one.

## 4. SFX LEXICON

Romaji + English translation in parentheses, exactly as written. 2-5 SFX per page.

### STAGE
- **KOTSU** (mic-tap) — a knuckle or a hand on the mic stand; the sound of a set beginning.
- **PAM** (palm-smack) — the harisen-less open-palm tsukkomi on the shoulder. **ONSTAGE ONLY.**
- **WAHHA** (whoo-haa) — the crowd mass. Hand-lettered, never bubbled. See 1.4.
- **KUSU** (a single giggle) — one person. Tiny, alone, white space around it.
- **SHIIN** (dead air) — hollow outlined letters on a white panel. Max twice per chapter.
- **DOTTO** (the wave) — the room breaking all at once, half a beat after the line.
- **ZAWA** (murmur) — sixty people turning over between bits.
- **PACHI-PACHI** (clap-clap) — applause. When nine people clap, write **PACHI... PACHI**.

### VENUE
- **GATAN-GOTON** (rattle-clack) — the 21:40 express overhead. The series' metronome.
- **POTSU** (one drip) — the arch sweating in August. Always a single drop, never rain.
- **GACHA** (kachak) — the theater door; also the capsule-hotel key drawer.
- **KII** (creak) — one of the sixty seats. Used when the house is nearly empty.
- **JII** (hum) — the mic live before anybody speaks. The loneliest sound in the book.
- **KACHIN** (clack) — the brass padlock on the mic case. Heard closing until ch54.

### NIGHT TOWN
- **PIRORIRO** (chime-lilt) — the 00:04 last-train departure chime. They never catch it.
- **PIN-PON** (bing-bong) — the GRAN PORTO call bell. Rung by Komako far too often.
- **KOO** (low hum) — the capsule hotel corridor at 3 a.m. Gaku's writing weather.
- **GAKON** (thunk) — the MARUMO truck's lift gate hitting the stop.
- **SHAKI-SHAKI** (crunch-crunch) — frost on the embankment gravel, January chapters only.
- **TON-TON** (tap-tap) — the drink-bar pitcher set down on a tray by Higuchi.
- **BASA** (fwump) — a stack of flyers dropped on a table.

### BODY / FEELING
- **`...`** (the held silence) — **THE SERIES' HEARTBEAT EQUIVALENT.** Not an SFX at all: an
  **empty aside space**, written as `- (Name, aside): ...` and drawn as three tiny floating
  dots beside the head where a mutter would be. It is the sound of a person choosing not to
  say the thing. **Max twice per chapter.** This device replaces every romance SFX in the book.
- **SU** (an intake) — a breath taken before a line. The only freely usable feeling sound.
- **KOKU** (a swallow) — used at most once per arc, and never by the same lead twice running.
- **HYU** (a small exhale) — reserved: Tokita Kenzō's nose-exhale in a public hall, ch53-55.
- **DOKI** (badump) — **MAXIMUM TWICE IN 55 CHAPTERS.** Never in a confession-adjacent scene,
  never on a two-shot, never with a blush. Both uses are the showrunner's to schedule. If a
  chapter wants DOKI, it wants the held silence instead, and it is wrong.

caption, or dialogue — not once, not at the finale.

## 6. MUTTERED ASIDES

Unbubbled text floating beside a head. 2-4 per page (engine law). The series' comedy engine and
its confession system at the same time.

**RULES**
1. **No bubble, ever.** Tiny lowercase text beside the head, occasionally a small dashed bubble.
2. **Six words maximum.** No exceptions.
3. **Always true.** An aside is the only place in this series where a character cannot lie.
4. **Never acknowledged.** Nobody hears them. Nobody responds. Nobody looks over.
   **ONE EXCEPTION IN THE WHOLE SERIES — locked to CHAPTER 44.** Someone answers an aside they
   were not supposed to hear, and it is the loudest event in 55 chapters. Everywhere else:
   zero. A writer who spends it in any other chapter has broken the series.
5. **Max 3 per chapter per character.**
6. Asides never state a feeling. See the rewrite rule (section 0).

**THE GRAMMATICAL SPLIT (this is what makes them unswappable):**
- **Komako's asides are FRAGMENTS.** No complete sentence, ever. Noun phrases and verdicts.
  Slow, plain, startlingly precise — the analyst nobody suspects. Usually contains a number,
  a body part, or one of his objects.
- **Gaku's asides are COMPLETE SENTENCES.** Subject, verb, the lot — inside six words. Warm,
  addressed to nobody, **always about her timing, never about her face.** He may never describe
  how she looks; he may only describe what she did to a room.

**EXAMPLES — Komako (fragments)**
- `he cut two words. mine.`
- `his cuff's buttoned. he's fine.`
- `three days. hasn't sat down once.`
- `a hole. he wrote me a hole.`

**EXAMPLES — Gaku (complete sentences)**
- `she gave them a whole second.`
- `nobody teaches that. she just does.`
- `the room turned. she turned it.`
- `she came in early. she was right.`

## 7. BANNED

Pillars' list, folded together with this file's. Any of these in a breakdown is a rejection,
not a note.

**WORLD**
- No magic, no system, no fantasy layer, no dream sequence longer than one panel.
- No cartoon executives, no sabotage, no "industry destroys art," no "heart beats math."
  The industry and the duo both fight with real moves only, and the numbers are always correct.
- Illness is never terminal. Parents are never dead for leverage. **The elders' divorce is
  never fully relitigated on-panel** — pieces, sideways, and one thaw. No flashback explaining it.

**ROMANCE**
- **No love triangles. Ever.** No romantic rivals at any point. The scout wants her talent, the
  rivals want their crown, nobody wants their hearts.
- **No tsundere denial constructs.** Neither lead ever denies a feeling, because neither is
  asked by the other, because both already know.
- No accidental gropes. No beach or hot-spring filler. No amnesia. No drunk confessions. No
  rain almost-kisses. No airport or station sprints. No "I've never had anyone be kind to me
  before" — nobody in this cast is that unguarded.
- **THE LEADS NEVER MISUNDERSTAND EACH OTHER.** External misreads (tabloid, family, fans) are
  legal. Between the two of them there is only the unsaid, never the mistaken. If a scene's
  tension dissolves the moment one of them learns a fact, the scene is illegal — rewrite it so
  the tension is one of them choosing not to say a fact they both already hold.

**CRAFT**
- **No monologue over 4 bubbles.** A punchline, a train, or Shachō's phone interrupts. Always.
- No bubble over 8 words. No page under 8 bubbles or over 14. No silent pages.
- No bit summarized in a caption when it could be performed in panels.
- No boke line that is merely random (see 1.3). No joke whose punchline is that she is stupid.
- No DOKI-type SFX beyond the two scheduled uses; none confession-adjacent.
- No aside acknowledged by another character, except the one reserved instance.
- No character stating an emotion in dialogue. Rewrite as timing or an object.
- No gag built on humiliation. Nobody is ever the butt.

**VIOLENCE**
- On-stage tsukkomi contact is craft: open palm, shoulder, in rhythm, PAM.
- **Offstage, nobody hits anybody, ever, for any feeling.** Not comedically, not once.

## 8. CHAPTER CHECKLIST

A breakdown that fails any box goes back to the writer before a single page is drawn.

- [ ] **1. Cold open mid-performance.** Page 1 opens on stage mid-bit, or mid-day-job
      "performance" — a mover's chant, a 3 a.m. front-desk absurdity, a flyer pitch. Never on
      waking up, never on scenery, never on an establishing shot.
- [ ] **2. At least one bit performed on-page**, in panels, using the bit unit (1.1) or a
      kakeai run (1.2). Not referenced. Not summarized. Performed.
- [ ] **3. A domestic beat with real page time** — food, laundry, a bandage, a bus ride, dishes,
      a bento, an umbrella, taping a box. Minimum three pages of the chapter live in it, and it
      is never a cutaway.
- [ ] **4. One correct money number**, from the recurring canon (¥1,000 ticket, ¥299 drink bar,
      ¥9,800 shift, ¥1,050/hr, the ten-ticket NORMA, ¥690/¥740/¥790 hamburg, ¥10,000,000
      prize), used correctly for the chapter's point in the timeline.
- [ ] **5. One gag run** from section 5 (one to three; never four), each either advancing a beat
      or costing somebody money.
- [ ] **6. The comedy page comes BEFORE the knife page.** The chapter must be funny first. A
      chapter that opens sombre and stays sombre is rejected regardless of quality.
- [ ] **7. Industry beat: zero or one, and it pays a character.** At most one piece of showbiz
      machinery per chapter, cut against a real object (3.5). The stage and the 3 a.m. table
      are the meal; the industry is seasoning.
- [ ] **8. Address ladder respected.** Correct rung for the chapter number, stage forms onstage,
      offstage forms offstage. No unscheduled given name. Ever.
- [ ] **9. Chapter-end pull present, and the title only makes sense after reading.** The last
      page lands on a small hook, a held breath, or an unanswered line — and the title is a
      phrase that was innocent on the contents page and is not innocent now.

**PLUS the engine's mechanical lint** (`scripts/lint-breakdown.ps1`): 25-32 pages, 6-8 panels
per page, exactly one splash, 8-14 bubbles per page, every bubble 8 words or fewer, at least
three [CHIBI] panels dropped mid-conversation, CONTINUITY NOTES present. Drawing never starts
on a failing breakdown.
