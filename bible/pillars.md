# AIBIKI — SHOWRUNNER PILLARS (locked)

アイビキ / AIBIKI — *Double Act*

This file is the constitution. Bible files (series.md, cast.md, style.md, arc.md) expand it;
nothing in them may contradict it. PURE ROMCOM: no magic, no system, no fantasy layer of any
kind. The drama engine is stage lights, money, timing, and two people who have both known for
seven years and both said nothing.

**STRICTLY 55 CHAPTERS. One complete story. Chapter 55 is the finale and it lands.**

STANDALONE LAW: this series shares nothing with any other property of this studio or anyone
else's — no shared universe, names, places, props, gags, tone, or cover motif. Its world is
showbiz-nocturnal: basements, stage lights, last trains, 3 a.m. family restaurants. It is
LOUD where other things are quiet.

---

## LOGLINE

Seven years ago, at partner-picking day in comedy school, they were the two nobody chose.
Today AIBIKI is the best unknown manzai act in the city: she is a once-a-decade live wire who
cannot write a word, he is a timing savant who cannot perform a line, and on a 60-seat stage
under a rail embankment they share one microphone and one bank balance and finish each
other's sentences for a living. Everyone asks. They always answer the same way: "We're
colleagues." The industry's founding horror story — the legendary duo that married each other
and died on stage within a year — happens to be the two old people who run their agency and
their home theater, who have not spoken in twenty-three years, and whose wreckage our two
leads commute between every single day.

TAGLINE: "On stage they finish each other's sentences. Off stage they can't start one."

## THE TWO LEADS (names locked)

**HAYASE KOMAKO (早瀬狛子)** — 24. The boke. Named after her great-grandmother; has been
introducing herself as "yes, like an eighty-year-old" since she was five, which is where the
timing started. A once-a-generation stage animal: she reads a room like weather, commits to a
bit with her whole skeleton, and can resurrect a dying set with one ad-lib — and she cannot
write ANYTHING. Blank paper frightens her more than any audience. Day job: crew at MARUMO
MOVING — she can carry a refrigerator alone, and has carried Gaku offstage under one arm
(it started as an emergency; it is now their signature exit). Public face: the loudest,
fastest, most fearless person in any room. Private face: she rereads their old flyers in the
bath; she has known the other meaning of "aibiki" since the day she suggested the name; she
has a savings envelope marked TROPHY SUIT (for the day he needs a real suit for the final)
that she has never told him about and adds ¥1,000 to after every gig, including the ones
that pay ¥0.

**TOKITA GAKU (時田学)** — 25. The tsukkomi and the pen. Writes every word of every set in a
capsule-hotel back office between 1 and 6 a.m. (day job: night front desk, CAPSULE INN
COMPASS — the guests don't know they're material). A timing savant: he counts laughs in
quarter-seconds, knows the 9:40 express passes over the theater mid-set and writes the pause
into the script. Alone on stage he reads as an angry librarian; with her he is the sharpest
tsukkomi of his generation. Public face: dry, exact, unimpressed, economical. Private face:
he keeps a field notebook titled OTHER PEOPLE'S LAUGHTER — she is forbidden to read it —
which the industry assumes is joke research. The last thirty pages are not jokes. They are
seven years of one-line entries logging her best ad-libs, dated, like a man keeping a ledger
of the thing he cannot say.

They are 24 and 25. Working adults. Nobody is in school. Combined comedy income at chapter 1:
about ¥120,000 a month, before the ticket quota eats it.

## THE NAME (canon, and the series' engine)

Partner-picking day, seven years ago: the leftover two, at a GRAN PORTO family restaurant,
flat broke. Cheapest thing on the menu: the AIBIKI HAMBURG STEAK, ¥690 — "aibiki" as in
mixed mince, two cheap meats, better ground together. She pointed at the menu and said:
"Us. That." He registered — in the same quarter-second he times everything — that "aibiki"
written another way is the old word for a lovers' secret meeting. He said nothing. She
watched him decide to say nothing, understood everything, and also said nothing.
**Both have known for seven years. Neither has ever said it. That is the series.**

## THE WALL — why no confession for 55 chapters (structural, never a misunderstanding)

There is no secret between them and no misunderstanding anywhere. The wall has four faces:

1. **The precedent they commute between.** ŌTORI SHIZUE (58) and SUGA TETSUJI (60) were
   GEKKŌ ("moonlight") — the greatest manzai duo of their era. They married; the act died in
   fourteen months; the divorce is the industry's founding cautionary tale. She now runs
   ŌTORI PRO, the agency AIBIKI is signed to. He runs GEKIJŌ GAKEPPUCHI ("Theater
   Cliff-Edge"), the 60-seat arch-basement that is AIBIKI's home stage. They have not spoken
   in 23 years. Our leads are contractually raised by one half of the wreck and artistically
   raised by the other. They don't discuss why they never date. They just both refill their
   glasses whenever anyone mentions Gekkō.
2. **Neither exists solo.** Her genius has no shape without his structure; his structure has
   no pulse without her lightning. The act is both livelihoods, both dreams, and the only
   thing either has ever been good at. To confess is to bet BOTH careers on the relationship
   outliving what killed their gods.
3. **The good part.** They already have what couples envy — perfect timing, a shared
   language, every night side by side. Both privately believe dating is how you trade the
   sacred thing for the ordinary one and lose both. They watched it happen. See face 1.
4. **The deadline (arc engine).** The FUTTONDA-1 GRAND PRIX ("F-1," ¥10,000,000, the
   national young-duo title) is open only to acts within TEN YEARS of formation. AIBIKI
   formed seven years ago. The series spans their years eight, nine, and ten. At ~ch31 the
   almost-confession is converted, over a chore, in under twenty words, into the PACT:
   **"After the final. Not before."** The heart gets scheduled behind a trophy — which is a
   promise, and a prison, and a clock.

The wall is love-shaped on all four faces. It NEVER runs on secrets, jealousy plays,
overheard half-sentences, or anyone dating anyone else.

**Reveal/knowledge schedule (locked):**
- Ch1: the reader learns both leads' private faces (the envelope, the notebook) — the leads
  learn nothing new; they have known for seven years. Dramatic irony is the reader's alone.
- Ch5: the ache is locked. The chapter where the reader understands that BOTH know, both
  have always known, and both have decided the act matters more. No flashback dump — it
  surfaces through the hamburg ritual and one wrong quarter-second of silence on stage.
- Ch11 (arc 1 finale): a TV scout tries to take her solo. She says no before he can finish
  the sentence. Gaku finds out from the scout, not from her. First real fight; first time
  the walk home happens in silence; the daruma's blank eye watches them not talk.
- ~Ch31: the PACT (see above). From here both feelings are surfaced fact between them —
  spoken around, never spoken — and the series becomes two professionals carrying one lit
  fuse together.
- Ch44 (arc 4 finale): the act nearly dies of success (her solo TV rise, his block, the
  tabloid misread). They choose the duo again, scarred, and re-sign the pact with a date on
  it: last eligible year.
- Ch55: the final. The set they perform IS the confession, disguised as material — the
  audience laughs at every true line. They win or they don't (arc agent proposes; showrunner
  leans WIN). Afterward: the same GRAN PORTO booth as chapter 1, the aibiki hamburg (¥790
  now), and the actual words, delivered as a manzai tag, mid-chore of splitting the bill.
  **Final image (locked): the theater's tip-jar daruma gets its second eye painted.**

## THE FIVE ARCS (boundaries + milestones locked; arc agent details rows)

1. **BASEMENT YEARS** (ch1–11) — the Gakeppuchi circuit: ticket quotas, day jobs, 3 a.m.
   writing sessions, the cohort of rival duos, the elders' cold war as weather. Milestones:
   ch1 cold-open mid-set (a bombed set, and why they keep going); ch5 ache locked; ch11 the
   solo-scout refusal + first silent walk.
2. **THE CIRCUIT** (ch12–22) — climbing: the agency showcase, theater-regular audition, a
   15-second street-interview clip that goes viral for the wrong reason, first TV crumbs.
   Milestones: ch17 their first televised 90 seconds (cut to 40); ch22 they qualify for
   F-1's quarterfinal bracket — and the prize money board goes up on Gaku's wall.
3. **YEAR EIGHT** (ch23–33) — the F-1 run, attempt one: they lose in the semifinal,
   beautifully, to the genius rivals — a loss that is also the first time the industry
   learns their name. Milestones: ch28 the semifinal; ch31 THE PACT; ch33 the famires
   waitress says her first full sentence of the series and it lands like a verdict.
4. **THE COST** (ch34–44) — success attacks the duo: TV wants her solo (panel shows love a
   loud woman; they have no use for her writer), his block, the tabloid misread (external
   only — the leads always hold the truth together), the cohort duo quits and its ex-boke is
   HAPPIER as a salaryman, which is the knife. Milestones: ch38 the friends' duo dissolves;
   ch41 her solo offer with a number on it (¥ per episode > their year); ch44 they choose
   the duo, full price, pact re-signed with a date.
5. **YEAR TEN** (ch45–55) — last eligible year: the run, everything pays, the elders thaw
   one rung ahead of the leads (Shizue walks down the arch stairs at ch54 — 23 years — and
   sits in the back row of Gakeppuchi). Ch54: the sacred relic — Suga unlocks the case and
   hands them GEKKŌ's old center mic for their last home-stage set; "Are you two—?" is asked
   one final time and neither answers. Ch55: the final, the set-as-confession, the 4-second
   pause where the 9:40 express would be (the broadcast hall has no train; they leave the
   pause anyway; the judges never know; we do), the famires booth, the words, the daruma's
   second eye.

Milestone beats above are LOCKED. Everything between them belongs to the writers' room.

## THE OPPONENT IS WEATHER, NOT A VILLAIN

The industry is a spreadsheet wearing a lanyard: never wrong, never understanding. Ratings,
ticket quotas, "shorts KPIs," a scout's polite math. Nobody is evil: the TV scout is right
that she'd kill solo; the agency's young manager is right that clips feed the theater; the
tabloid writer is just filling Tuesday's page. BANNED: cartoon executives, sabotage,
"industry destroys art" straw men, "heart beats math." The duo survives by REAL moves —
better sets, smarter clips, a fanbase built one trucker at a time — and the math is always
shown honestly with correct recurring numbers.

## GEOGRAPHY (canon)

**DOTEMACHI (堤町)** — a west-side terminal neighborhood defined by a brick rail embankment;
the private line runs overhead and the town lives in its arches: a tool shop, a standing bar,
a print shop, and GEKIJŌ GAKEPPUCHI — 60 seats, arch ceiling, sweating brick, a tip-jar
DARUMA with one painted eye by the door, and the 9:40 express that passes directly overhead
mid-first-set (veterans write the pause; amateurs get flattened). Ten minutes east: the
terminal, the all-night GRAN PORTO family restaurant (their office: two drink bars, ¥299 each,
six hours), and CAPSULE INN COMPASS where Gaku night-desks. The last train leaves at 00:04
and they have never once caught it: the nightly 40-minute walk home along the embankment is
the series' talking road. Big-city TV land is 25 minutes away and might as well be abroad.

## ENSEMBLE (7 + relic — cast agent builds full sheets)

1. **ŌTORI SHIZUE** — 58, agency president, ex-boke of GEKKŌ. Runs 40 acts with zero
   sentimentality and one desk drawer she never opens. Speaks fluent contract; laughs, when
   ambushed by something truly funny, in an enormous unguarded bark she visibly resents.
2. **SUGA TETSUJI** — 60, owner of Gakeppuchi, ex-tsukkomi of GEKKŌ. Waters the pavement,
   guards the case with GEKKŌ's center mic, has watched every set from the sound booth for
   30 years. Grades sets by how he polishes glasses. Has not said Shizue's name since 2003.
3. **The rival duo "geniuses"** — two-man act, one savant weirdo + one glacial craftsman;
   friendly-vicious; they beat AIBIKI at ch28 and spend two years afraid of them.
4. **The cohort duo (the friends)** — formed the same day at the same school; always one
   rung ahead, then one behind; they dissolve at ch38 and the ex-boke becomes a genuinely
   happy salaryman, which nobody in the cast recovers from quietly.
5. **The manager** — 27, Ōtori Pro's overworked digital native; loves them via spreadsheet
   (clip views, conversion, "vertical video is a door, not a betrayal"); the modern industry
   with a human face.
6. **The famires night waitress** — 40s, GRAN PORTO. Has watched seven years of 3 a.m.
   writing sessions from behind the drink bar. Speaks perhaps ten lines a year. Each one is
   a verdict. (Her ch33 line is a locked milestone.)
7. **The trucker** — long-haul driver, recurring 3 a.m. capsule-hotel guest; heckled their
   flyer once, became their first true fan; follows results from highway service areas;
   the audience-of-one the series keeps cutting to. At the ch55 final he is IN the hall,
   in his good jacket.
+ **THE RELIC** — the daruma at the theater door (one eye painted since before anyone can
  remember; tips go in its box; its blank eye is the series' witness) and GEKKŌ's mic in
  the locked case. Objects carry what nobody says. No pets, no mascots.

Gaku's father: a man who has never once laughed at his son's profession — not hostile,
just unlaughing; he pays off at ch53–55 (one exhale through the nose in a public hall,
which in this family is a standing ovation). Komako's family: loud, suburban, warm, think
she "works in events"; they find out from television at ~ch41.

## RUNNING GAGS (seed early, pay late — style agent expands; these are locked)

1. **The hamburg ritual.** Every real decision in seven years has been made over GRAN
   PORTO's aibiki hamburg. Price climbs with the years: ¥690 → ¥740 → ¥790. Ch55 pays it.
2. **The daruma's blank eye.** Present in the background of every Gakeppuchi chapter.
   Second eye painted on the final page of ch55. Nobody may hang a lampshade on it.
3. **"We're colleagues."** Simultaneous, flat, to every interviewer, relative, and rival,
   across 53 chapters. Ch54: asked one last time; neither answers; the silence is the
   biggest line in the series.
4. **OTHER PEOPLE'S LAUGHTER.** The forbidden notebook. She finally reads it in the ch50s;
   what's in it is locked (see leads). Until then it appears often and is never opened
   on-panel.
5. **The express pause.** The 9:40 train over the theater; the 4-second hole Gaku writes
   into every first set; pays at ch55 in a hall with no train.
6. **The offstage carry.** When a set dies, she carries him off under one arm. Started as
   emergency, now signature; the crowd thinks it's a bit. The one time it happens OFFSTAGE
   (ch44, out of a TV building) it is not a bit at all.
7. **The trophy suit envelope.** Her secret ¥1,000-per-gig savings for his final-day suit.
   He finds the envelope at ch52 and says nothing — the second person in this series to
   know a thing and hold it.
8. **Bit-bleed.** When a true feeling surges on stage, the audience laughs harder — they
   think it's material. The crowd laughing at the true thing is the series' knife. Ration:
   once per arc, maximum.

## VOICE TEMPERATURES (locked so writers can't drift)

- Komako on stage / in public: fastest voice in the room, fearless, physical, boke logic
  that turns out to be secretly correct one beat too late. Komako private/asides: slow,
  plain, startlingly precise — the analyst nobody suspects ("he rewrote line four. for me.").
- Gaku on stage / in public: dry, exact, clipped tsukkomi arithmetic. Gaku private/asides:
  full warm sentences addressed to no one, always about her timing, never about her face.
- Shizue: fluent contract with proverb-shaped clauses. Suga: glass-polishing silences,
  then one sentence that has been aging for years.
- Manzai bits render ON the page: kakeai in 4–8 word bubbles is the house rhythm; a bit is
  never summarized in caption when it can be performed in panels.
- The chapter-end pull is law: every chapter ends on a small hook, a held breath, or an
  unanswered line. Chapter titles must only make full sense after the chapter is read.
- COLD-OPEN LAW: every chapter opens mid-performance — on stage, mid-bit, or mid-day-job
  "performance" (a mover's chant, a front-desk 3 a.m. absurdity). Never on waking up,
  never on scenery.

## SEASONS ARE THE CLOCK

~2.5 years across 55 chapters: years eight, nine, ten of a ten-year eligibility window.
The showbiz calendar is the seasonal engine: New Year TV specials, spring showcase, summer
open-air flyer wars, the F-1 bracket every autumn-winter. Heat in the arches in August;
breath visible on the embankment walk in January. Named venues, named trains, named menu
items, real prices — always. Specificity is the weapon against sounding like AI.

## BANNED (on top of the engine's bans)

- No magic, no system, no fantasy, no dream sequences longer than one panel.
- No tsundere denial constructs; no accidental gropes; no beach/hot-spring-trip filler; no
  amnesia; no love triangles EVER (no romantic rivals at any point — the scout wants her
  talent, the rivals want their crown, nobody wants their hearts).
- No "I've never had anyone be kind to me before." Nobody here is that unguarded.
- No monologue over 4 bubbles — a punchline, a train, or Shizue's phone interrupts.
- No drunk confessions, no rain almost-kisses, no airport/station sprints.
- The leads never misunderstand each other. External misreads (tabloid, family, fans) are
  legal; between the two of them there is only the unsaid, never the mistaken.
- Illness is never terminal; parents are never dead for leverage; the elders' divorce is
  never fully relitigated on-panel — we get pieces, sideways, and one thaw.
- On-stage tsukkomi strikes are craft (open palm, shoulder, rhythm); NOBODY hits anybody
  offstage, ever, for any feeling.
- The industry is never sabotaged and never sabotages. Both the duo and the machine fight
  with real moves only.
