# AIBIKI — CAST BIBLE

Expands `bible/pillars.md`. Contradicts nothing in it. Everything here is drawable in flat
black ink + screentone + white paper (see `engine/art-spec.md`) and speakable in 4-8 word
bubbles (see `engine/chapter-engine.md`).

Names in this file are the ONLY legal speaker names in a breakdown.

---

## 1. WORLD RENDER — baseline block

Render-relevant canon only. If it cannot be inked flat, it is not in this section.

### 1.1 THE STAGE WARDROBE (locked — the act's entire visual identity)

**MISMATCHED FORMAL.** One suit, worn two ways. This is AIBIKI's costume for all 55 chapters
and it never changes cut, only condition.

- **GAKU — the suit, correct.** Charcoal single-breasted three-button, fastened at the middle
  button only, white shirt, thin black tie in a small tight knot. Trousers break clean over
  plain black shoes. Cuffs *buttoned*. Rendered: jacket and trousers = flat 40% dot
  screentone, one flat black tone-shape for the lapel shadow; shirt = pure white paper; tie =
  solid black. He is a vertical gray column with one black stripe down it.
- **KOMAKO — the same suit, wrong on purpose.** The jacket is *his old one* — the one he grew
  out of upward at nineteen — a size too big in the shoulder, sleeves rolled to a fat double
  cuff that sits below the elbow. White shirt, top button open. Trousers hemmed short above
  the ankle over white socks and **black steel-toe work boots**, the same boots she moves
  refrigerators in, polished for stage. Rendered in the identical 40% tone as his, on purpose:
  in any two-shot they are one gray mass with two different silhouettes.
- **THE UNTIED BOW TIE (locked visual).** A black bow tie hangs loose and untied around her
  open collar at every single performance. She cannot tie one and has never asked anyone to
  show her. Drawn as two flat black ribbons down the shirt front. It is untied in every
  chapter it appears. The day it is tied is a plot event; the showrunner schedules it.
- **F-1 / showcase addition:** a small white paper entry badge safety-pinned to the left
  lapel, hand-numbered. Both wear it. It is the only white thing on the suit besides the shirt.

### 1.2 STREET / DAY-JOB WEAR

- **KOMAKO — MARUMO MOVING.** Navy coveralls, 30% tone, white oval MARUMO patch on the left
  chest, black lettering, legible. Wide black lumbar support belt, worn high. White towel
  around the neck May through September. **State-switch:** on shift = zipped to the throat;
  off shift = top half stripped and the sleeves knotted at her waist over a white tank. The
  artist reads her shift status from the sleeves alone, in every panel.
- **GAKU — CAPSULE INN COMPASS.** Thin flat-black nylon vest over a white shirt, white COMPASS
  patch on the back, clip-on plastic badge on the chest reading **TOKITA / NIGHT**, always
  crooked, always crooked the same way, low on the right. Off duty: gray zip hoodie over a
  white tee, flat canvas shoulder bag riding on the hip.

### 1.3 GEKIJŌ GAKEPPUCHI — VENUE RENDERING CANON

- **Arch brick = texture, never architecture.** 25% dot screentone across the arch, with
  hand-inked mortar lines on only the two courses nearest the reader; the rest stays tone.
  Never draw a complete brick wall — the art spec forbids full environments in more than two
  panels per page, and this room has to be legal in all of them.
- **The sweat.** August chapters: three to five short white flick-marks running down the tone,
  the wall drips. January chapters: tone drops to 15% and breath is drawn as white puffs.
- **THE SPOTLIGHT IS NEGATIVE SPACE.** Never draw a light source. Draw the *absence of tone*:
  a hard-edged white pool with the screentone stopping dead at its rim. Anything inside the
  pool loses its background tone entirely. Stepping out of the light = the tone closes over
  them. This is the single most important render rule in the series.
- **The crowd.** A solid black silhouette bank across the bottom of the frame, white gaps for
  teeth and eye-highlights. No individual faces — **except Ōgaki the trucker, who is the one
  face in the bank given full detail, always.** That is how the reader finds him.
- **Crowd laughter lettering.** A hand-lettered mass, never bubbled. Grammar in `style.md`.
- **The sound booth.** Upper rear: a black rectangle with one white window. Suga's glasses are
  the only detail visible in it. A cut to the booth is a cut to two white ovals.
- **THE DARUMA.** Door-side, hip-height, on an upturned beer crate. Solid black body, flat
  white face, **one eye painted, his left and our right; the other a blank white circle.** Tip
  box beside it: taped cardboard, a cut slot, hand lettering. The blank eye faces the room in
  every Gakeppuchi chapter, in the background, unremarked. Nobody lampshades it. Ever.
- **THE MIC CASE.** Battered black flight case under the booth: two chrome latches, a
  constellation of cigarette burns across the lid, one brass padlock. Inside, unseen until
  ch54: GEKKŌ's chrome ball-head center mic, a strip of white tape on the stand reading
  **GEKKŌ** in Suga's handwriting. The case is drawn closed in every chapter until it isn't.

## 2. LEAD 1 — HAYASE KOMAKO (早瀬狛子), 24

The boke. Named for her great-grandmother, who sold taiyaki off a cart on the Dotemachi
shopping street and used the same three-word pitch for fifty years without changing a syllable.
Komako has been saying "yes, like an eighty-year-old" since she was five, which is where the
timing started. A once-a-generation stage animal: reads a room like weather, commits with her
whole skeleton, can resurrect a dying set with one ad-lib — and cannot write a word. Blank
paper is the only thing on earth that frightens her. Crew at MARUMO MOVING, ¥9,800 a day,
can carry a refrigerator alone.

### PUBLIC/STAGE FACE

The loudest, fastest, least frightened person in any room, and she gets there first — before
the producer finishes the sentence, before the silence sets, before anybody can decide the room
is going badly. Her volume is not confidence, it is *coverage*: she fills every gap so that no
gap can be measured. She talks to strangers like they owe her money in a friendly way. She
takes the heavy end of every object without being asked and without appearing to have decided
to. On stage she plays a woman completely certain about things she clearly invented four
seconds ago, and she never blinks first. Interviewers adore her and get nothing out of her.
Rivals warm up standing near her the way you stand near a stove. When a set dies she does not
look sad; she picks her partner up under one arm and walks him off, and sixty people think it
is a bit.

### PRIVATE FACE

Slow. Plain. Startlingly precise. She rereads their old flyers in the bath until the ink runs,
and can tell you which ones were printed the week they were nearly evicted. She has known the
other meaning of *aibiki* since the afternoon she suggested the name, has known that he knows,
and has spent seven years deciding, over and over, that the act is the bigger thing. Her
private register never reaches for a feeling; it reaches for *evidence* — a line rewritten, a
wrist that clicks, a pause moved two beats later. **The envelope:** a kraft-brown pay envelope
marked **TROPHY SUIT** in her handwriting, corners gone soft, a pencil tally down one side.
¥1,000 goes in after every gig, including the ¥0 ones — that is the entire point of it. It
lives in a rice-cracker tin under the passenger seat of the MARUMO two-ton. He has never been
told. He finds it at ch52 and says nothing, which makes him the second person in this series
to know a thing and hold it.

### VOICE — four registers

1. **STAGE (boke).** The fastest voice in the series. Total conviction, zero hedging, present
   tense. Her nonsense is always *secretly correct one beat late* — the audience gets it after
   Gaku's retort, and that lag is where the laugh lives. She never says "maybe," "sort of,"
   "I think," or "eh?" on stage. Not once in 55 chapters.
2. **PUBLIC OFFSTAGE.** Same speed, less shape. Overrides silence. Answers questions nobody
   asked. This is the register that says "We're colleagues" in perfect unison with him.
3. **PRIVATE (with Gaku, offstage).** Speed drops by half. Sentences go short, flat, accurate.
   Numbers appear. She stops performing certainty and starts reporting facts, and the facts
   are always about *him and the work*, never about herself.
4. **ASIDE (unbubbled).** Slowest of all. Six words maximum. The analyst nobody suspects: an
   observation with a conclusion welded to it, and the conclusion is always right.

**SAMPLE LINES — stage**
- "I'd like to return this Tuesday."
- "Escalators are just very patient stairs."
- "That's not a line. That's grief."
- "Sir, your soup has a strategy."
- "I'll play the corpse and the widow."
- "Fridges don't have handles. They have opinions."
- "Then I proposed to the vending machine."
- "Ask me about horses. Go on. Ask."

**SAMPLE LINES — offstage**
- "The bill's ¥1,598. You paid last."
- "Your handwriting gets small when you're tired."
- "Don't lift that. Your wrist clicks."
- "The express ran nine seconds early."
- "You wrote it. I just shouted it."
- "Buy socks. I've seen the current ones."
- "I'll take the heavy end. Always have."
- "Sit down. You've been standing since four."

### ARTIST LOCK

158cm, compact-powerful, built by four years of other people's furniture: short neck, wide flat
shoulders, thick forearms, low center of gravity — draw the torso as a square block, never a
taper. Hair is **solid flat black**, thick, cut to the jaw by her own hand, heavy blunt fringe;
in public she pins the right side back with **two crossed black bobby pins forming a small X
above the temple**, in every stage panel. Eyes large and round, heavy upper lash line, one tick
under the outer corner, **two stacked white highlights, one big one small**, in every public
panel. **Private micro-difference, mechanical: one highlight only, dropped to the lower half of
the iris.** She wears the stage jacket rolled to a fat double cuff with the bow tie hanging
untied, and the MARUMO coveralls knotted at the waist off-shift. Signature props: the kraft
**TROPHY SUIT envelope**, private, in the cracker tin — and, visible in nearly every offstage
panel, a **1.8m black ratchet cargo strap slung across her body like a bag strap, chrome buckle
at the hip.** She carries everything with it, including him. **PRIVATE-FACE TELL:** the pins
come out, the black hair falls across her right eye, and both palms go flat on the table. She
stops gesturing entirely. Pins out = private register, every time.

## 3. LEAD 2 — TOKITA GAKU (時田学), 25

The tsukkomi and the pen. Son of a one-man blade-grinding shop; grew up watching a man put an
edge on other people's knives and never once smile at the result. Writes every word of every
set at the CAPSULE INN COMPASS night desk between 1 and 6 a.m., ¥1,050 an hour, on a clipboard
balanced on the key rack. Counts laughs in quarter-seconds. Knows the 21:40 express passes over
the theater mid-first-set and writes the four-second hole into the script. Alone on stage he
reads as an angry librarian; standing next to her he is the sharpest tsukkomi of his generation.

### PUBLIC/STAGE FACE

Dry, exact, unimpressed, economical — a man who treats a joke as a load-bearing calculation and
is quietly appalled that everyone else treats it as a mood. He does not sell his lines; he
*files* them, and the room laughs anyway because the arithmetic is perfect. He apologizes to
staff, never to audiences. He argues about set lists with the affect of someone disputing a
utility bill. In public he says "We're colleagues" flatly, at the same instant she does, and
then he checks the time — that is his whole public personality and it is enough. Nobody in the
industry has ever seen him excited. Two people have seen him satisfied. The rival craftsman is
one of them, which is why the rivalry has teeth.

### PRIVATE FACE

Warm in full sentences, addressed to nobody, always about her timing and never about her face.
He is the only person alive who knows exactly how good she is, in numbers, and the numbers are
the closest he can get to saying it. He eats standing up. He keeps three ¥1,000 notes folded
in the notebook's back cover for her taxi if a shift runs long. **The notebook:** A6, black
board cover gone round at the corners, elastic band, stub pencil in the elastic, a strip of
white tape on the front reading **OTHER PEOPLE'S LAUGHTER** in his hand. The industry assumes
joke research; the industry is right about the first two hundred pages. The last thirty are
one-line dated entries logging her best ad-libs, seven years of them, a ledger kept by a man
who cannot say the thing so he audits it instead. She is forbidden to read it. She never has.
He has known what *aibiki* also means since the quarter-second she pointed at the menu.

### VOICE — four registers

1. **STAGE (tsukkomi).** Clipped arithmetic. Short retorts that name the exact error in her
   logic, delivered as a correction, never as an insult. Frequent second person, frequent
   numbers, no exclamation. His funniest register is his most bureaucratic one.
2. **PUBLIC OFFSTAGE.** Fewer words than the stage voice. Flat. Answers only what was asked,
   and when possible answers with a figure. Ends conversations by looking at a clock.
3. **PRIVATE (with Komako, offstage).** Still economical, but now the sentences are *for her* —
   instructions that are care in disguise. Eat. Sit. Take the boots off. He gives her craft
   notes as a way of standing close to her.
4. **ASIDE (unbubbled).** The one place he uses full warm sentences. Always about her timing,
   an object, or the room. Never about how she looks. Never the word "her" plus an adjective.

**SAMPLE LINES — stage**
- "You cannot return a day, ma'am."
- "Nobody proposes to a vending machine."
- "Grief takes a ticket number now?"
- "Pick one. Corpse or widow. Choose."
- "That fridge has no opinions. Neither do you."
- "Which one was the horse question?"
- "Stairs are stairs. Please sit down."
- "Hold. ...And there's our train."

**SAMPLE LINES — offstage**
- "Eat first. Fourteen boxes today, right?"
- "You held it three-quarters. Don't fix it."
- "Line six is yours. Wreck it."
- "The quota's ten. We've sold six."
- "Don't apologize to the microphone again."
- "Take the boots off. Twelve hours."
- "I moved the pause two beats later."
- "Sit on the left. Your knee."

### ARTIST LOCK

184cm and narrow — a vertical rectangle with long forearms, the neck pushed forward by ten
thousand clipboard hours, no taper at the waist. He is a line, she is a block, and that reads
at thumbnail size. Hair is **mid-gray 40% screentone**, straight, overgrown, no product, three
flat forehead spikes, the back past the collar — against her solid black it is the fastest
two-shot ID in the book. Eye grammar is opposite to hers: **narrow, half-lidded, upper lash
line only, no lower line, one small square highlight** where she gets two round ones. He wears
the stage suit *correctly*, cuffs buttoned, knot tight, its tail 2cm too short, which he has
never noticed; day job is the COMPASS vest and the crooked TOKITA / NIGHT badge. Signature
props: the **OTHER PEOPLE'S LAUGHTER notebook**, elastic and stub pencil, never opened
on-panel — and **the counting tic, drawn mechanically: his left thumb taps the second knuckle
of his index finger four times, four tiny arc-ticks off the thumb, while the square highlight
vanishes and the eye goes flat black.** Thumb ticking plus dead eye means he is timing.
**PRIVATE-FACE TELL:** he unbuttons exactly one cuff — the left — and pushes that sleeve to the
forearm. One arm only. Both cuffs buttoned is public; left sleeve up is private, notebook
face-down beside him.

## 4. SUPPORTING CAST

### 4.1 ŌTORI SHIZUE (鳳静枝), 58 — president, ŌTORI PRO. Ex-boke of GEKKŌ.

Ran the greatest manzai act of her era for eleven years, married her tsukkomi, and watched the
act die in fourteen months. Now runs forty acts out of a third-floor office with a broken
buzzer and does it without one gram of sentiment: she signs, she books, she cuts, she is never
unfair and never warm. She has read every contract in the industry and can quote clause numbers
across three agencies. **The drawer:** bottom-left of her desk, never opened on-panel, never
explained. She rests her heel against it while she talks.

- **PUBLIC LINE:** the fluent contract voice — clauses shaped like proverbs, terms instead of
  feelings, and a cigarette she does not light indoors.
- **PRIVATE LINE:** when something is genuinely funny she is ambushed by an **enormous
  unguarded bark laugh** — one panel, mouth wide, all teeth, completely undefended — and she
  visibly resents it for the next two panels. She has never once apologized for it and has
  never once let it happen twice in a chapter.

**SAMPLE LINES**
- "Clause nine. Read it before crying."
- "Talent is a receipt, not a promise."
- "Bring me a number, not a feeling."
- "Sign here. Regret is not billable."
- "Forty acts. Two of you eat."
- "You're funny. That's the smallest problem."
- "GWAHHA— ...That was the air conditioning."

**ARTIST LOCK.** 165cm, straight-backed, square-shouldered; reads as a filing cabinet in a
jacket. Hair is **flat white** — the third value in the book, so all three principals separate
instantly — cut short and pushed back off a high forehead, one hard black tone-shadow under the
fringe. Eyes narrow with a single long horizontal highlight; heavy lower lid line, the only
character with one. Wears a boxy black jacket (solid black, no tone) over a white shirt
buttoned to the throat, no jewelry except a men's watch worn face-in. Props: a fountain pen
capped and uncapped as punctuation, and an unlit cigarette she rolls between two fingers. The
bark-laugh panel is drawn full-face and enormous, teeth white, eyes shut — the only time her
eyes close in the series. Her office is rendered as one desk edge, one phone, and tone.

### 4.2 SUGA TETSUJI (須賀哲二), 60 — owner, GEKIJŌ GAKEPPUCHI. Ex-tsukkomi of GEKKŌ.

Waters the pavement outside at six every evening whether it has rained or not. Has watched
every set from the sound booth for thirty years and grades them by how he polishes glasses:
fast circles = you rushed it; a long stare at the glass = you had something and dropped it;
setting the glass down unfinished = the best thing he has seen this year, and he will not say
so. Guards the mic case. **Has not said Shizue's name out loud since 2003.**

- **PUBLIC LINE:** eight words a night, maximum, most of them about the door takings.
- **PRIVATE LINE:** glass-polishing silence, then one sentence that has been aging in a cask
  for a decade and comes out at full strength.

**SAMPLE LINES**
- "Sixty seats. You filled nine. Congratulations."
- "You rushed the hole. The train waits."
- "That was funny. Don't do it again."
- "The daruma keeps the count. Not me."
- "Lock the case. Don't touch the latch."
- "I have been polishing this since 2003."
- "Wipe your own glass tonight, Hayase."

**ARTIST LOCK.** 172cm, thick through the middle, sloped shoulders; drawn as a soft rectangle.
Hair **flat mid-gray tone**, thinning, combed wet and flat across; heavy black brows are his
loudest feature. Glasses are square, drawn as **two flat white ovals with no eyes visible in
90% of his panels** — his eyes appear only three times in the series and each is an event.
White short-sleeved shirt, black apron with a bar towel through the strings, forearms bare and
scarred by thirty years of cable. Props: the glass and towel (always, even outdoors, even in
the street), and a green plastic watering can with a long spout. He is drawn from behind more
than any other character; the reader knows his back better than his face.

### 4.3 THE RIVAL DUO — **KANNAKUZU** (鉋屑, "wood shavings")

Formed at a different school, two years senior, and the best two-man act under thirty in the
country. They beat AIBIKI in the F-1 semifinal at ch28 and spend the following two years
genuinely, publicly afraid of them. Friendly-vicious: they buy the drinks, they mean the
compliments, and they would take the last rung off the ladder behind them without breaking
stride. The name is a craftsman's joke — a plane makes a beautiful curl of waste to leave one
true surface behind. Konuma named them. Nukui has never asked what it means.

#### NUKUI SŌTA (温井想太), 26 — boke. The savant weirdo.

Does not rehearse and cannot explain what he does. Speaks to everyone as if resuming a
conversation from a previous life. Turns up at Gakeppuchi on nights he is not booked and stands
at the back. Obsessed with Komako in a way that is entirely craft and never romantic — he wants
to see what she does when the floor breaks, so he breaks floors. Eats one convenience-store
egg sandwich a day and considers this a complete system.

**SAMPLE LINES**
- "I don't rehearse. I remember forward."
- "Your Tuesday bit. I dreamt it first."
- "Do it again, but hate me."
- "Hayase. You're the only real weather."
- "There's a wasp in the lighting rig."
- "I broke the floor. She stood up."
- "Keep the tape. I'll take the loss."

**ARTIST LOCK.** 170cm, boneless posture, stands with his weight on one hip and his hands
hanging like he forgot them. Hair **solid black with a wide white shine band** — deliberately
close to Komako's value so their two-shots feel wrong, and separated by shape: his is long,
soft, tucked behind both ears, half a step past needing a cut. Eyes enormous and perfectly
round with **three highlights**, the only three-highlight character in the book; he almost never
blinks, so the artist draws them open in every panel including chibi. Stage suit is the standard
KANNAKUZU dark suit worn with the jacket over one shoulder like a coat. Prop: a folded paper
crane he makes and unmakes from receipts, mid-conversation, without looking at it. He is drawn
too close to whoever he is speaking to, always half a body-width nearer than is comfortable.

#### KONUMA REIJI (小沼玲司), 29 — tsukkomi. The glacial craftsman.

Does not write jokes. Writes *floors* — structures precise enough that Nukui can jump anywhere
and land. Times to the frame, keeps a hard notebook like Gaku's and knows it, has read every
AIBIKI set list Gaku ever posted and once corrected one by e-mail, unasked, at 4 a.m. Respects
Gaku enough to be cruel to him accurately. Never raises his voice, on stage or off.

**SAMPLE LINES**
- "Your fourth line is two words heavy."
- "We measured you. You're close. Not yet."
- "I don't write jokes. I write floors."
- "Congratulations. Sincerely. Now go home."
- "Tokita. Page eleven. You cheated once."
- "Don't thank me. I'd have cut you."
- "Two years. Then we stop being polite."

**ARTIST LOCK.** 180cm, flat-shouldered, absolutely still; when others move he is the panel's
fixed point. Hair **flat 20% light-gray tone**, short, side-parted with a razor line, no shine
band at all — he is the only character with no white in his hair, so he reads slightly dead in
a group shot. Eyes: single narrow highlight, upper and lower lash lines both drawn thin, brows
almost level. Wears the KANNAKUZU suit correctly and one degree better than Gaku wears his —
same silhouette, sharper lapel, and a **white pocket square folded flat**, which is the visual
insult. Props: a slim steel mechanical pencil clicked exactly once before he speaks, and a
folded set list he never looks at. He stands with both feet planted and never shifts weight.

### 4.4 THE COHORT DUO — **ASHITA KARA HONKI** (明日から本気, "serious from tomorrow")

Formed the same afternoon as AIBIKI, at the same school, in the same room of leftovers. Named
by two seventeen-year-olds who thought it was funny and then had to say it into a microphone for
nine years. Always one rung above AIBIKI, then one rung below, then two. They dissolve at ch38.

#### MIZOBE KANTA (溝辺寛太), 25 — boke. The one who gets out.

Big, cheerful, generous, and the only person in the cohort who has never once been cruel about
anybody's set. Overclaims constantly and everybody lets him. After ch38 he takes a job at a
building-materials wholesaler, gets a desk, gets eight hours of sleep, and becomes visibly,
uncomplicatedly happy — and nobody in this cast recovers from it quietly. He still comes to
shows. He laughs the loudest in the room now that it costs him nothing.

**SAMPLE LINES**
- "We're doing fine. We're doing great."
- "Fifteen tickets. My mum bought eleven."
- "Serious from tomorrow. Always tomorrow, right?"
- "I sleep at eleven now. Eleven."
- "My desk has a drawer. A drawer."
- "I still laugh. Just not professionally."
- "Don't look at me like that, Hayase."

**ARTIST LOCK.** 178cm, heavy and round-shouldered, the widest silhouette in the cast. Hair
**solid black, cropped very short with a squared hairline** — a black cap shape, no shine band,
instantly distinct from Komako's jaw-length block. Round face, small round eyes, single
highlight, and the only character drawn with a permanent smile line at each corner of the mouth.
Stage wear: a shiny cheap two-tone jacket in 60% tone that photographs badly, which is the point.
**After ch38 the render changes once and permanently:** the jacket is replaced by a plain white
shirt and a lanyard, and he is drawn five kilos heavier and sleeping-well, with the smile lines
deeper. Prop pre-38: a plastic bag of unsold tickets. Prop post-38: a company lanyard he fiddles
with when AIBIKI are on stage.

#### SUEHIRO NOBORU (末広昇), 26 — tsukkomi. The one who is left.

Wrote the name. Did the quotas. Booked the rooms. Believed hardest and longest, and is the one
holding the microphone when it stops. He does not become bitter — this series does not do
bitter — he becomes *careful*, which is worse to watch. Stays in the industry in a smaller way.

**SAMPLE LINES**
- "Ten years is a long joke."
- "I wrote our name at seventeen."
- "Say the name. Out loud. Go on."
- "You two got the good half."
- "I'll do the quota. You rehearse."
- "Tokita. Tell me it was worth it."
- "Fine. Then we're finished. That's fine."

**ARTIST LOCK.** 175cm, thin, tense; drawn with his shoulders slightly up around his ears in
every panel after ch30. Hair **mid-gray 40% tone** like Gaku's — deliberate, because they are
the same kind of man and only one of them got the right partner — but parted hard on the left
and kept neat, where Gaku's is overgrown. Eyes: single round highlight, thick lower lash line,
faint tone-shadow under both eyes from ch23 onward that never goes away. Wears the same shiny
two-tone stage jacket as Mizobe, buttoned all the way, and offstage a windbreaker with the
school's old comedy-course logo half peeled off. Props: a clipboard of ticket tallies, and a
lighter he flicks without a cigarette.

### 4.5 KUJIRAI SATSUKI (鯨井さつき), 27 — manager, ŌTORI PRO

Two years in, handles nine acts, has never performed and never wanted to. Digital native in a
building with a fax machine. She loves AIBIKI genuinely and expresses it exclusively through
spreadsheets: a shared tab per act, retention curves, a colour-coded row for "rooms that
actually converted." She is never wrong about the machine and never quite hears the room. She
is not a villain and never becomes one; at ch41 she brings Komako the solo number because it is
her job to bring it, and she cries in the stairwell afterwards where nobody sees, and she still
brings it.

- **PUBLIC LINE:** KPI dialect delivered deadpan, at speed, with total sincerity.
- **PRIVATE LINE:** the same dialect, quieter, applied to something that cannot be measured —
  which is the only time she sounds young.

**SAMPLE LINES**
- "Vertical is a door, not a betrayal."
- "Clip-through's four percent. That's alive."
- "I made you a tab. Please look."
- "Can you bomb again, but framed?"
- "Row fourteen is your funniest row."
- "Thirty seconds. Then the algorithm leaves."
- "I love you two in aggregate."

**ARTIST LOCK.** 160cm, small, upright, moves in short fast steps. Hair **flat black in a low
tight bun** with a pen through it — a black sphere silhouette, unmistakable next to Komako's
black block. Round glasses drawn as thin circles with the eyes visible through them (opposite of
Suga), single highlight, permanently slightly wide. Wears an oversized gray blazer over a plain
tee and trainers, ŌTORI PRO lanyard, tote bag with a folded tripod sticking out. Props: a phone
held horizontally in both hands like a steering wheel, and a laptop she opens on any flat
surface including the daruma's crate. In chibi she is drawn as bun-plus-glasses-plus-laptop only.

### 4.6 HIGUCHI SETSU (樋口節), 46 — night waitress, GRAN PORTO

Has worked the 22:00-06:00 shift at the terminal-side GRAN PORTO for eleven years and has
watched seven of them happen in booth 12. Knows what they order before they sit. Knows which of
the two pays. Rings the drink-bar refills without being asked and has never once mentioned the
six-hour occupancy of a ¥299 seat. Her plastic name tag is scratched through the last letter so
it reads **HIGUCH**; the leads have called her "onēsan" for seven years and do not learn her
name until ch33.

- **HARD RULE — her grammar is the character.** Before ch33 she speaks **only in fragments**:
  noun phrases, prices, times, never a subject and a verb together. Roughly ten lines a year.
  **Ch33 is her first complete sentence in the series** (locked milestone) and it lands as a
  verdict because the reader has waited 33 chapters for a predicate.

**SAMPLE LINES (pre-ch33 — fragments only)**
- "Two drink bars. Six hours. Again."
- "Hamburg's ¥740 now. Don't argue."
- "Closing the grill at five."
- "That one. The first version."
- "Booth twelve. Same as always."
- "Ice machine's dead. Sorry."

**CH33 VERDICT (locked):** *"Seven years. Never once the same side."* — factual, about seating,
not about feelings; pays at ch55 when one of them sits down on the wrong side of the booth.

**ARTIST LOCK.** 163cm, square and unhurried; the only adult in the series who is never in a
rush. Hair **solid black, scraped back into a short flat tail under a paper cap**, two loose
strands at the temple that are drawn identically in every panel for eleven years of night
shifts. Eyes half-lidded, no highlight at all — she is the only character in the book drawn with
**zero eye highlights**, which is why her one raised eyebrow is enormous. GRAN PORTO uniform:
white shirt, dark apron in 40% tone, the scratched HIGUCH tag, a black order pad in the apron
pocket she almost never writes on. Props: the tray, held flat against her hip like a shield, and
the drink-bar pitcher. She is nearly always drawn behind or beside the booth, never seated.

### 4.7 ŌGAKI TSUTOMU (大垣勉), 51 — long-haul driver. The first true fan.

Drives a four-ton refrigerated box for HINODE TRANSPORT on the west-side night runs and stays at
CAPSULE INN COMPASS six or seven nights a month. Heckled their flyer once, seven years ago,
outside the arch — "who are you two supposed to be?" — got an answer he did not expect from a
girl holding a cargo strap, and has come to Gakeppuchi ever since. Follows their results from
service areas at 3 a.m. Calls them both "sensei," which mortifies Gaku and delights Komako. He
is the audience-of-one the series cuts to. At the ch55 final he is in the hall, in his good
jacket, which does not fit him.

- **PUBLIC LINE:** blunt, warm, entirely without self-consciousness; talks to strangers about
  the two of them the way other men talk about a ball club.
- **PRIVATE LINE:** the postcards. He mails them from service areas to **AIBIKI, c/o GEKIJŌ
  GAKEPPUCHI**, five to eight words on the back, never about the act — about the weather, a
  mountain, a vending machine that took his money. Suga tapes every one to the sound-booth wall
  without comment. By ch55 the wall is full.

**SAMPLE LINES**
- "Watched it at Kusunoki. Twice. Loud."
- "I heckled you once. I apologize regularly."
- "Postcard's got a mountain. That's all."
- "Sensei. Both of you. Sit down. Eat."
- "Nine hundred kilometers. Laughed at three hundred."
- "Bought two tickets. I'm one man."
- "Rig's called Number Four. She listens."

**ARTIST LOCK.** 176cm, thick-necked, barrel-built, permanently sunburned on the right forearm
only (driver's tan — drawn as tone on one arm, white on the other, in every panel). Hair
**flat mid-gray tone**, cropped, with a white towel band; heavy stubble drawn as a tone shape,
never as hatching. Small eyes, single highlight, deep smile creases. Wears a HINODE TRANSPORT
short-sleeved work shirt untucked, a belly warmer, and driving gloves with the fingers cut off,
which he does not remove indoors. Props: the postcards, and a thermos of instant coffee. His
rig, **"YONBAN" (Number Four)**, carries a hand-painted andon light box over the cab reading

establishing shot. He is the ONE detailed face in the crowd silhouette bank, always.

## 5. RELATIONSHIP MAP — series start

### 5.1 THE KNOWLEDGE GEOMETRY (from pillars — absolute)

**Both leads have known for seven years. Neither has ever said it. Nobody in this series ever
discovers anything.** There is no secret to uncover, no misunderstanding to clear, no third
party holding information. The tension is one hundred percent **the unsaid**, never the unknown.

- **The leads:** each knows their own feeling, knows the other's, and knows the other knows.
  All three layers were settled at the GRAN PORTO table seven years ago and none has ever been
  spoken. They do not avoid the subject; they *work around* it the way you work around a load-
  bearing column — daily, expertly, without ever mentioning the column.
- **The reader:** learns both private faces in ch1 (the envelope, the notebook). The dramatic
  irony belongs to the reader alone and it never converts into a "reveal" between the leads.
- **The elders:** know exactly what they are looking at, from the inside, and have opposite
  policies about it. Shizue's policy is contractual distance. Suga's policy is silence and
  door takings. Neither will say the useful sentence, because saying it would require saying
  the other's name.
- **Everyone else:** asks. Every interviewer, relative, rival and fan asks the same question
  across 53 chapters and gets "We're colleagues," flat, in unison. At ch54 it is asked one last
  time and neither answers, and the silence is the biggest line in the series.
- **THE ONE STRUCTURAL RULE:** if a scene's tension can be resolved by one character learning a
  fact, the scene is illegal. Rewrite it so the tension is one character *choosing not to say a
  fact both already have.*
