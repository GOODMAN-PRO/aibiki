# ARC 1 — BASEMENT YEARS (ch1–11)

**Late January to mid-June, year eight.** The Gakeppuchi circuit: quotas, day jobs, 3 a.m.
writing sessions, the cohort, and the elders' cold war as weather.

**IN-STATE:** the best unknown act in the city, opening a sixty-seat bill because it sold the
fewest tickets on it, ¥2,300 down
on the night, seven years in, and nothing said.
**OUT-STATE:** the same act, forty minutes of silence in its history, a scout's card in a
pocket, and a clock on a poster that both of them have already counted.

## CH 1 — "CARRIED"

**Premise.** Cold open at 21:41, mid-bit, opening the bill in the express slot: THE
ELEVATOR. Gaku is a man riding to the ninth floor; Komako gets in and presses every button,
because "on every floor there's someone waiting who doesn't know it yet." At ninety seconds
the 21:40 express arrives
exactly where Gaku wrote it — four seconds of overhead thunder, one wide panel, both of them
staring at the ceiling while sixty seats' worth of people who live here stare up too — and the
pause lands perfectly for the first time in weeks. Then the set bombs anyway, and the reason is
arithmetic: the house is thirty-one, twelve of whom walked out after the act before them, row A
is one production scout (NIWA, Sakuragawa) asleep with his mouth open, and row B is four rival
acts taking notes with their pens moving and their faces still. The only laugh in the room is
polite — three white gaps, evenly spaced, exactly on the beat, from people being kind. Loop three
detonates into nothing. And then Komako carries him off under one arm on the exit, as she has for
four years, and THAT gets the biggest laugh of the night: the only thing that killed was the
thing they never wrote. In the green room Suga's brown pay envelope has **31** on the flap and
**−¥2,300** under it, and where the money should be there is one word in his handwriting. Neither
of them mentions the word. Both of them see it.

**BIT — THE ELEVATOR (perform in panels).** Loop 1: she presses all nine. Loop 2: she explains
the building's feelings floor by floor while he audits her ("that's four things; pick one"). The
hole: floors passing in silence, four seconds, nobody speaks, the express. Loop 3: the doors open
on six and a stranger actually gets in — she was right, one beat late, as always. Tag: split
between them, and it is a good tag, and nobody laughs at it.

**Domestic beat.** 07:30 the next morning, Marumo yard, a four (no elevator), a refrigerator she
takes alone on a strap while Asai watches; ¥9,800 counted into her hand at 17:00. That night, his
desk at Compass, 23:00–08:00, ¥8,400, the guest who checks in at 3:40 in a wedding suit, Ōgaki
asleep in 41. And between them, at 01:00: GRAN PORTO, booth 12, two drink bars at ¥299, **one**
aibiki hamburg at ¥690 with two forks because it is the end of the month — the ritual performed
in full, its origin not explained, not even glanced at. On the walk: the four-in-a-row, potage
¥130, the coffee he holds and never drinks and hands over full. The 47 Steps: they sit on step 12,
because the set died, and the reader does not yet know that is what step 12 means.

**Ground gained.** None, and that is the point — the chapter's job is to show the reader a wall at
rest. The only movement is one millimetre: she notices, in an aside, that he rewrote line four to
hand her the vindication beat, and says so flatly to nobody; he hears it and does not answer.

**Industry beat.** One: NIWA asleep in row A. He is not a villain and he is not rude. He paid.

**Final page.** Sunday morning, the Dead Arch. A man from Insatsu Hamada pasting the F-1 poster
over three layers of dead flyers, including theirs. ENTRIES OPEN 25 JULY · ¥2,000 · OPEN TO ACTS
THROUGH YEAR 10. Gaku stops walking. His thumb ticks four times against his knuckle; his aside is
three numbers. Komako arrives with two cans, hands him the coffee, doesn't look at the poster, and
says one word: **"Three."** Last panel: the poster's corner already lifting in the wind, and
underneath the fresh paste, from last year's flyer, one visible eye.

## CH 2 — "MISPRINTS"

**Premise.** Cold open mid-performance on the street: Komako running a forty-second bit at a man
who is trying to reach an escalator, selling him a ¥1,000 ticket he will buy and not use. It is
February, the dead month, the bill is Friday, and they have sold three of ten — which is a
¥7,000 hole with their name on it. At Insatsu Hamada they order 500 flyers at ¥3.80, ¥1,900, and
Hamada charges them for 480 as he has for five years, because the Riso lays its diagonal streak
across the last twenty sheets of every run and always will. Gaku redesigns the flyer and puts her
name first for the first time in seven years; she sees it on the proof, says nothing about it,
and asks Hamada for one extra copy she will never hand out. They sell seven on the night and eat
¥3,000. And the chapter's real payload is for the reader only: she takes the streaked twenty home
and flattens them against the bath tiles until the ink runs; he takes his home to a box under the
bed where seven years of them are stacked in date order; neither has ever told the other; both
have been doing it since year one.

**BIT — THE FLYER.** Gaku hands Komako a flyer. She receives it as a legal summons and
cross-examines it for four minutes: who authorised this, is the date binding, what is her
liability. He audits her ("that's four legal terms and none of them are correct"). The hole falls
in loop two, dead centre. Loop three vindicates her: the flyer IS binding, because he printed the
norma terms on the reverse by mistake and has therefore committed them both to ten tickets in
writing. Tag lands. The room is thirty-eight and it is a decent night.

**Domestic beat.** Coin Laundry Suisei, 02:10, dryer 4 because dryer 4 runs hot. Their stage
suits going round; her folding his old jacket — the one she wears on stage — with a competence
that has nothing to do with tenderness and reads as nothing else.

**Ground gained.** He moves her name to the front of the flyer. She buys a second copy of it.
Neither act is mentioned by either of them at any point in the chapter, then or ever.

**Industry beat.** None.

**Final page.** His room, 05:40. The box comes out from under the bed. This week's streaked flyer
goes on top of four hundred and eight others. On the underside of the box lid, in his hand, a
running count, updated tonight. His aside is a number: **"Four hundred and nine."** Cut, last
panel: her bathroom, a wet flyer stuck to the tile with his face still legible and hers gone, and
a kraft envelope on the edge of the sink with a fresh tally mark. Two ledgers, one gig apart,
counting the same seven years in different currencies.

## CH 3 — "COUNT THE HOUSE"

**Premise.** Cold open at the Compass desk, 03:40: a guest wants to check out and check back in
to catch the ¥2,900 after-two rate, and Gaku — performing patience for an audience of one — does
the arithmetic aloud, finds it saves the man ¥500, and processes it. Ōgaki is in 41, back off a
900km run, and buys two tickets on the spot ("I'm one man"). Friday's house is **three**. Three
people in a sixty-seat theater in February; Suga looks at the door for a long moment and does not
cancel and does not comp anyone in. They do the full four minutes at three people, because the
alternative is not doing them. Ōgaki laughs like a room — one man making the noise of forty — and
the other two laugh because he does, which is a different sound and everyone on stage can hear
the difference. Gaku counts eleven laughs and privately grades four of them real. Afterwards
Ōgaki tries to explain why the third service area got him and gives up halfway and stands there
holding his ticket stubs.

**BIT — THE NIGHT DRIVE.** Gaku announces he wants to drive through the night. Komako plays every
service area he stops at, each needier than the last: the first offers coffee; the second asks
him to stay a bit; the third has been waiting since 1998 and remembers what he ordered last time
and what he said and who he said it about. Loop three vindicates her opening claim that service
areas are the loneliest buildings in Japan. Tag: split, four words each.

**Domestic beat.** Bentō Hachiya after nine, red sticker, karaage bento ¥290 eaten standing on
the steps outside arch 8; she gives him the karaage and takes the rice, because he does not eat
while he is still counting, and she has known that for six years.

**Ground gained.** He writes the third service area's last line **for her voice** rather than for
the bit, and she performs it exactly as written instead of ad-libbing over it, which she has not
done in two years. Neither remarks. Suga, in the booth, stares into a glass a long time.

**Industry beat.** None. Ōgaki is not the industry; that is the entire point of Ōgaki.

**Final page.** Kusunoki SA, 03:00, northbound: Ōgaki writing a postcard on a steering wheel —
six words, none of them about the act. Cut forward three days: Suga taping it to the sound-booth
wall beside eleven others, without comment, without telling anyone. Last panel, Komako in the
doorway asking how many they need to sell to close the bill; Suga gives her the number; it is not
an encouraging number and he does not soften it.

## CH 4 — "ELECTRICITY"

**Premise.** Cold open at the Marumo yard, 07:30, minus one degree: the SAN-NI-ICHI / HAI chant,
and Komako filling the thirteen dead seconds of the tailgate lift with a bit for a crew of three,
because thirteen seconds of silence at that hour will kill a man. February cold snap. At the
theater the heater is off; Suga runs a bill at twelve degrees and tells the house to keep their
coats on and nobody complains, because they have all been here in February before. The daruma's
tip box is emptied onto the bar top and counted: **¥4,180**, against a bill that isn't ¥4,180.
Komako fixes the theater's heater with one bolt from Nejita Hardware (¥30, sold by weight) and
Suga lets her, which is the most affectionate thing he does all chapter. A young act asks him a
question about GEKKŌ — not maliciously, just young — and Suga polishes a glass and does not
answer, and both leads refill their glasses at the same moment without looking at each other, and
neither of them notices they did it.

**BIT — THE BILL.** Gaku wants to query his electricity bill. Komako IS the bill, in person, and
it has feelings, and it remembers November. Loop 1: she claims he left the heater on for nine
days. Loop 2: the bill escalates into a relationship, hurt that he only calls when he wants
something. Loop 3: the bill produces evidence and is correct — nine days — and the tsukkomi turns
into a confession about a heater. Twenty-nine in the house, coats on, breath visible in the
spotlight, which is the best-looking bit of comedy in the chapter.

**Domestic beat.** Corn potage ¥130 at the four-in-a-row, the coffee handover, gloves with the
fingertips gone. She darns the cuff of his old jacket on the last train platform they will not
board.

**Ground gained.** Komako pays Suga the ¥4,180 shortfall out of her own pocket in the alley and
asks him not to say anything. Gaku is in the doorway. He sees. He says nothing, and she knows he
saw, and neither will ever raise it — which is the physics of this series stated in one panel.

**Industry beat.** None.

**Final page.** After closing. Suga alone, counting the ELECTRICITY box. He takes Komako's ¥4,180
and does not put it in the till; he puts it in a different envelope, one with AIBIKI on the flap
and a column of figures going back years — everything he has quietly carried for them and never
collected. Last two bubbles: one sentence from Suga to nobody, aged about a decade before use.
Last panel: the total at the bottom of the column. It is a large number.

## CH 5 — "THE OTHER SPELLING" · **MILESTONE: THE ACHE IS LOCKED**

**Premise.** Cold open mid-set, opening the bill, a decent house of thirty-four, the bit already
running. LOST PROPERTY: Gaku has come to reclaim an umbrella; Komako is the clerk who requires
him to describe it. Loop by loop the required detail becomes impossible and then intimate — its
mood in rain, which side it leans, what it does when he is late — and Gaku answers each one
faster than he should, because the arithmetic is the only thing holding him up. Then, on the last
line of loop three, he comes in **a quarter of a second late** — he has not missed a beat in two
years — and says it not as the character but as himself: *"I'd know it in the dark."* The room
detonates. They hear material. They laugh harder at that line than at anything in the set, because
it is the only true sentence anyone has said in the building all night. Komako takes the laugh
and stretches it the way only she can, the tag lands on top of it, they walk off right, bowing,
and neither of them looks at the other on the five steps down. This is arc 1's single permitted
use of bit-bleed. There is not another one until arc 2.

Later, booth 12, 02:50. They killed, so it is **two** hamburgs tonight, ¥690 each, and neither
comments on the extravagance. In booth 9 a drunk reads their flyer aloud and makes the obvious
joke about the other way of writing *aibiki* — *sneaking off, eh, the two of you* — and laughs at
himself. **Neither lead reacts. Not a flinch, not a beat, nothing, in perfect unison.** And
Higuchi, setting down two glasses, sees both of them not react, and looks at them for one panel
longer than she has looked at them in eleven years, and says four words about the ice machine.
The reader now knows: both know, both have always known, and both just chose again, in the same
quarter-second, in front of a witness.

**BIT — LOST PROPERTY.** As above. Sixty umbrellas produced in loop three; the vindication is
that hers is the only one that matches, because she took it in the first place, which she claimed
at 0:20.

**Domestic beat.** Two hamburgs instead of one, and the arithmetic that permits it, said out loud:
¥1,380 plus ¥598 of drink bar for six hours of office.

**Ground gained.** Nothing moves, and that is the delta the reader feels: the wall is mutual and
load-bearing and both hands are on it. Concretely, one small thing does move — **he does not cut
the line from the set.** It stays in for the rest of arc 1, performed nightly, in front of
strangers, which is the only way either of them can stand to keep it.

**Industry beat.** None.

**Final page.** 04:40, the Compass back office, the fluorescent tube humming its B-flat. The
notebook, elastic off, opened away from the reader — never on-panel, not once in 55 chapters —
and the thickness of the section his thumb goes to. He writes one line and a date. His aside is a
full warm sentence about a quarter of a second, addressed to nobody, and it does not contain the
word "her." Last panel: the elastic going back on, exactly where it was.

## CH 6 — "DOUBLE BOOKED"

**Premise.** Cold open with four people on one microphone. The junior act MC-ing has misread
Suga's running order, and ASHITA KARA HONKI walk on while AIBIKI are ninety seconds into their
slot. Nobody stops. They play it: four performers, one mic, one stage 3.2m wide, and the best
five minutes of comedy the theater has seen all month, none of it written. Suga watches it
happen and stares into his glass for a very long time. Afterwards at Tachinomi Mimizuku — ¥390
highballs, free boiled eggs after two — the four of them argue about whether it counted: Suehiro
says nothing counts if it isn't written; Mizobe says nothing counts anyway; Komako says it
counted because Ōgaki laughed at the part they didn't plan. Seven years of shared history gets
laid down here without one line of exposition: same room, same afternoon, same leftovers, always
one rung apart. Then Mizobe leaves early for "a thing in the morning," and does not say what it
is, and Suehiro does not know there is a thing.

**BIT — THE DOUBLE BOOKING (improvised, and the page must show it being improvised).** Komako and
Mizobe compete to be the boke; Gaku and Suehiro try to tsukkomi the same line and collide;
Mizobe's overclaiming ("fifteen tickets, my mum bought eleven") becomes the frame; the vindication
is that the running order was correct all along and it was the MC who was wrong, which Komako
asserted in the first ten seconds. There is no written tag, so there is no tag — remember this on
the last page of ch11.

**Domestic beat.** March: the stage gets repainted by whoever closed the March bill, and ASHITA
KARA HONKI closed it, so it is theirs to do — and AIBIKI stay until 01:30 and paint anyway. Black
gloss, one roller between four. **A stripe of it goes onto the cuff of Gaku's old jacket, the one
Komako wears on stage, and it never comes out, and it is visible for the next forty-nine chapters.**

**Ground gained.** In the chaos, Komako reaches for Gaku's line and he lets her have it, then
takes hers. Eleven seconds of swapped roles, the first time ever. Neither mentions it. He writes
it down at 04:00.

**Industry beat.** None. A clerical collision is house business, not machinery.

**Final page.** The green-room wall at 02:00: four names in marker, two formation years, the same
year, written the same week. Suehiro copying the March slip deadline onto the back of his hand
because his phone is dead. Last panel: Mizobe's phone face-down on the counter at Mimizuku,
buzzing, and nobody picking it up.

## CH 7 — "THE TWELFTH"

**Premise.** Cold open at the door of a 300-seat hall: AIBIKI in full stage suits tearing tickets
and MC-ing a queue, because the bottom three acts on Ōtori Pro's SPRING SHOWCASE work
front-of-house, and they are twelfth of fourteen. They perform to a queue for forty minutes and
it is a real performance and Komako is magnificent at it. Then they go on twelfth, and they are
good, and a 300-seat room is a completely different animal: the laugh takes almost half a second
longer to arrive and comes back off the rear wall, and every single one of Gaku's quarter-second
marks is wrong, and he can hear it happening while it happens and can do nothing. Shizue watches
from the back for ninety seconds and leaves. Kujirai films the whole thing vertically from the
wings and is thrilled about a number. And it is **12 April** — the date on their formation card,
seven years to the day — and neither of them mentions it, and both of them know, and on the way
to the hall they pass Shingei Academy where partner-picking day is going on behind the glass and
they slow down and do not stop.

**BIT — THE SEATING CHART.** Komako is a guest convinced her seat number is a rank and behaves
accordingly toward row F. Gaku ushers, auditing ("that is a seat, not a peerage"). Loop three: a
no-show puts her in row A, vindicating her, in front of 300 people. Tag lands late, off the back
wall, and Gaku hears exactly how late.

**Domestic beat.** Hachiya is shut by the time they get back, so it is convenience-store egg
sandwiches on the 47 Steps. The set was good, so by the rule they should walk past step 12 — and
he sits down on it anyway. She notices what that means before he does, and sits down too, and
opens both sandwiches.

**Ground gained.** He tells her, plainly, "my count doesn't work in a room that size" — the first
time in seven years he has said out loud that there is something he cannot do. She does not
comfort him. She asks a technical question about the rear wall. That is how these two love each
other, and the chapter is built to make the reader feel the difference.

**Industry beat.** One: the showcase — ranked bill, Shizue's ninety seconds, Kujirai's clip.

**Final page.** Midnight, outside Shingei Academy. Lights off, the second-floor window, and
inside, a classroom where the chairs have been pushed into pairs — and two chairs left apart at
the back, exactly where they were left seven years ago today, because nobody ever tidies that
room properly. Three bubbles, no speech about it. Komako's is four words. Last panel: the two
chairs.

## CH 8 — "OPENING ACT"

**Premise.** Cold open mid-bit in a supermarket forecourt at 10:00 on a Golden Week morning: PA
feedback, a folding stage, a crowd of eleven people who are waiting for free tissues, and ¥15,000
for the act before the agency's twenty percent. The set goes **fine**, which is worse than
bombing, because fine is what a car park does to comedy. Then the third-Wednesday bingo at the
shopping-arcade community hall, ¥8,000 each, four years running, where every grandmother in the
room knew Komako's great-grandmother and her taiyaki cart. Komako does the old woman's
three-word pitch, unchanged in fifty years, and the hall comes apart — the biggest laugh in the
chapter, and it is not hers, and she knows it and gives it away anyway. Then a real Gakeppuchi
bill that same evening: three rooms in one day, and the reader gets to feel the whiplash of what
a working act actually is. Money shown on the page throughout: ¥15,000 becomes ¥12,000 after the
agency, becomes ¥6,000 each, becomes ¥5,000 after fares.

**BIT — THE SAMPLE LADY.** Komako as a demonstrator who cannot accept that the sample is free and
insists on invoicing for it. Gaku as the customer doing arithmetic on a toothpick of sausage.
Loop three vindicates her: the store is in fact recovering the cost in the shelf price, so the
sample has never been free, which she asserted at 0:12. It works in a forecourt, barely, and both
of them know exactly how much of it the wind took.

**Domestic beat.** She carries both PA speakers alone up the loading ramp, one under each arm.
He times it. He writes the time down. The number is as close as he gets to saying anything, and
the reader has now been taught to read his notebook without ever seeing inside it.

**Ground gained.** At the bingo she introduces him to the arcade as "my partner," the grandmothers
hear the other thing, and both leads say **"We're colleagues"** in flat unison — the gag at full
strength for the first time in front of people who genuinely love her. Then Komako's aside, six
words, slow and exact, and it is not a denial and not a confession; it is an accurate description
of a decision.

**Industry beat.** One: Kujirai turns up at the forecourt with a tripod and a spreadsheet and
tells them the clip from the queue at the showcase converted at four percent, and she is right,
and it is the first time the modern industry is kind to them.

**Final page.** The 47's passenger seat, 23:40. The rice-cracker tin, the kraft envelope, the
pencil tally down the side. ¥1,000 of the ¥5,000 goes in. Last panel is the tally column and how
far down the envelope it has already travelled. Her aside is one number, and it is the first time
the reader can compare it to something.

## CH 9 — "HE PUT THE GLASS DOWN"

**Premise.** The chapter where AIBIKI *kills*, and the reader finally understands what these two
are. Cold open at Marumo: a four, a wardrobe, and Asai frozen on a half-landing under a load he
cannot hold — so Komako performs at him, four seconds of pure nonsense delivered at a nineteen
year old's ear, and he laughs and his arms come back and the wardrobe moves. Nunokawa watches
and says nothing. That is where the set comes from. That night, house of forty-one, they run
FURNITURE and the room comes apart: panels tighten to eight a page, laughter mass runs across
the gutters, and one panel finds Ōgaki's face in the black bank of the crowd. Gaku is counting
laughs, reaches twenty-two, and **stops counting**, which he has never done. And in the sound
booth Suga stops polishing, looks into the glass for a long moment, and **sets it down
unfinished** — the highest grade in the building, given four times since 2010 — and says nothing,
and neither lead sees it. The reader sees it.

**BIT — FURNITURE.** Gaku is a man being moved house against his will; Komako is a mover who has
decided he is furniture and processes him accordingly: blanket-wrapped, strapped, inventoried,
stickered FRAGILE, discussed in the third person over his head. Loop three: she carries him — the
signature offstage exit folded into the material for the first time in seven years, so the carry
is now the punchline rather than the apology. The vindication: he is, in fact, easier to move
than the fridge, which she stated at 0:30 while he was objecting.

**Domestic beat.** Dryer 4 at 02:00, both of them sitting on the folding table, the flickering
tube at the far end, and ¥100 they do not have to have an argument about tonight.

**Ground gained.** She asks him to write the carry into the act permanently. He says, "Then it's
a bit. Not an exit." She says, "Good." Four bubbles, and what has actually been agreed is the
mechanism the entire series ends on: **a true thing is sayable in this act if it is called
material.** Nobody in the chapter notices they have agreed to anything.

**Industry beat.** None.

**Final page.** After closing, house lights on 70%, Suga wiping down alone. The unfinished glass
is still standing on the board where he left it. He walks past it twice. He does not finish it.
Three bubbles: one sentence from Suga, to nobody, that has been aging for years. Last panel: the
glass, the coaster wedged under fader four, the blank eye of the daruma in the background of the
frame, unremarked.

## CH 10 — "INTEREST"

**Premise.** Cold open at the Compass desk, 03:00, rain: Gaku performing infinite patience at a
guest who has lost a locker key, while the bucket in his own theater four kilometres away is
filling up. Rainy season. Everything is damp; the flyers curl; the stage suits will not dry and
dryer 4 has to run twice, which is ¥200. Then the arithmetic closes in from three sides at once:
a wet Saturday kills the bill, her back seizes on a double she takes anyway for ¥12,600 and
Nunokawa benches her for two days, which is ¥19,600 gone, and her **eleventh and final** Shingei
tuition payment falls due — ¥12,000 — and she pays it, and after seven years she is finished, and
she tells nobody. She is ¥6,000 short on rent, and there is ¥91,000 in an envelope in a tin under
a truck seat that she will not touch, and the reader is the only person on earth who can see both
facts at once. She borrows the ¥6,000 from Mizobe instead.

**BIT — THE COIN LAUNDRY.** Gaku is waiting on dryer 4. Komako is a woman who opens other
people's dryers "to check on them," on the grounds that clothes get frightened. Loop two: she
begins issuing welfare judgements on strangers' laundry. Loop three vindicates her — dryer 4 runs
hot, and somebody's shirt genuinely was burning, and she is the only person in the ward who
noticed because she is the only person who looks. Modest kill, twenty-six people, in the rain.

**Domestic beat.** The whole chapter is domestic: a bucket under a leak, a lumbar brace pulled
tight one notch further than last month, two hundred yen of dryer, and a borrowed ¥6,000 in an
envelope with an apology written on the flap.

**Ground gained.** He notices the back. He does not ask about it and does not offer to help,
because either would be unbearable for both of them. Instead he moves her blocking two steps left
so that in loop two she can put her weight on the mic stand for eleven seconds. He never mentions
it. She uses it every night for the rest of the arc. She never mentions it either.

**Industry beat.** One: Kujirai's spreadsheet. Clip-through is four percent, which is alive, and
she asks — sincerely, without malice, and with a straight face — whether they could bomb again,
but framed better next time.

**Final page.** Mizobe's loan envelope on the table at Mimizuku, ¥6,000 inside, and on the flap in
his handwriting: **PAY ME BACK IN DECEMBER** — a joke about a television he genuinely believes
they will be on. Komako's aside is three words. Last panel: Mizobe walking out into the rain
under the arch, in a jacket that is not a stage jacket, and neither of them looks at it long
enough to wonder why he owns it.

## CH 11 — "FORTY MINUTES" · **MILESTONE: THE SOLO SCOUT, AND THE FIRST SILENT WALK**

**Premise.** Cold open mid-set, opening the bill, mid-June, house of forty-four, running THE
INTERVIEW, and it kills. NIWA HISASHI is in the room — the same production scout who slept through chapter
one, sober this time, watching properly, and right about absolutely everything. Afterwards he
takes Komako aside by the daruma and begins the sentence: *"Have you ever thought about going out
on your—"* and she says **no** before he finishes it. Not angrily. Instantly, the way you step off
a kerb. He is not offended, he is not a villain, and he is not wrong: panel shows want a loud
woman, panel shows have no use whatsoever for a writer, and he can put her on television inside a
year, and he says so kindly, and leaves a card.

**Gaku does not hear any of it.** He learns it forty minutes later on the wet pavement outside,
from Niwa himself, who assumes he already knows, because why on earth wouldn't he.

**The first real fight, and it is not a misunderstanding.** He is not angry that she was asked. He
is angry that she answered **without asking him**, because in doing that she made a decision about
his life on his behalf and closed a door he was never allowed to look through; and underneath
that he is furious that she refused, and cannot say why he is furious, and she knows precisely
why and will not say it either. It runs under twelve bubbles total. It happens at the level
crossing in ninety-second instalments, and the barrier lifts in the middle of a sentence, and
neither of them picks the sentence back up.

**BIT — THE INTERVIEW.** Komako is a job applicant who answers every single question in the first
person plural. Gaku is the interviewer trying to establish who "we" is, auditing her ("there is
no 'we' on this form"). Loop three: asked to name her greatest weakness, she completes a sentence
he started in loop one, having waited three minutes to do it. It is the best four minutes they
have done all arc — **and it is the reason the scout wants her, which the reader works out before
either lead does.**

**Domestic beat.** The forty minutes. She buys potage; he buys the coffee; when hers is empty he
does not hand his over, and it goes cold in his hands, and he carries it the rest of the way.
Neither sits on step 12, because sitting would mean talking. She does not walk backwards.

**Ground gained.** The wall flexes hard and holds, and — for the first time — both of them feel it
hold. Gaku also learns something he did not want: she will burn her own career for this act
without hesitating. That is not a relief. It is a debt he can never repay and never asked her to
extend.

**Industry beat.** One: Niwa.

**Final page.** Sankaku Bridge. The set killed, so there is a tag, and it is a good one, and
neither of them says it. They part left and right. Then: 01:20, Gakeppuchi, Komako back alone to
collect the cargo strap she left behind, house lights on 70%, sixty empty seats, the daruma on
its crate with its one painted eye and its one blank one facing the room. She says the tag out
loud. Both halves — his and hers. Last panel: nobody answers.

# ARC 3 — YEAR EIGHT (ch23–33)

**Mid-November, year eight, to March, year nine.** The F-1 run, attempt one. They lose in the
semifinal, beautifully, and it is the first time the industry learns their name.

**IN-STATE:** quarterfinalists with no express slot, a number on a wall, and a pact that does not
exist yet.
**OUT-STATE:** a name people say, a fuse lit and scheduled, and a verdict delivered by a woman who
has been holding a tray for eleven years.
**LOCKED MILESTONES:** ch28 (the semifinal loss to KANNAKUZU) · ch31 (THE PACT) · ch33 (Higuchi's
first full sentence).

- **CH23 — "ELEVENTH OF TWELVE."** Quarterfinal, night two of three, streamed, five judges,
  ticketed strangers: they draw the eleventh slot in a tired room and Komako spends the first
  forty seconds simply waking six hundred people up, on purpose, at cost to the set.
- **CH24 — "TWELVE OF THIRTY-SIX."** They advance, 36 down to 12, and celebrate by ordering the
  same thing they always order — and Higuchi says "hamburg's ¥740 now," which it has been for a
  fortnight, and neither of them had noticed, because neither of them has looked at that menu
  since they were eighteen years old.
- **CH25 — "STAND CLOSER, PLEASE."** Three interviews in one day; "We're colleagues" four times
  in eight hours; and a photographer asks them to stand closer together for the frame and they do
  it immediately, professionally, without a flicker, and it is the most unbearable page in the
  arc.
- **CH26 — "THE CHAIR."** Shizue and Suga are in the same building for eleven minutes and never
  once in the same room; the leads are sent back and forth carrying a chair, a clipboard and a
  key between two people who will not speak, and are used as furniture by their own gods.
- **CH27 — "NINE VERSIONS."** The week before the semifinal: the four minutes are rebuilt nine
  times, the argument is whether to leave a four-second hole in a hall that has no train, Komako
  says leave it because the audience will lean in for a train that never comes, and Gaku leaves
  it in for a reason he gives her and one he does not.
- **CH28 — "BY A HAIR."** *MILESTONE.* The semifinal, 1,600 seats, no cameras, industry in the
  balcony: they are magnificent and KANNAKUZU are better by a hair; twelve go in, four come out,
  AIBIKI are fifth; and Konuma finds Gaku in the corridor afterwards and tells him precisely
  which hair, kindly, in two sentences, because he respects him enough to be accurate.
- **CH29 — "THE STATION WE NEVER USE."** The same night: the hall empties, the corridor lights
  go off behind them section by section, they walk to a station neither has ever used and take a
  train for once — and the set had a tag, and it was a good one, and they say it, and saying it
  is somehow worse than the silence in chapter eleven.
- **CH30 — "SOUND OFF."** The televised final watched from booth 12 with the volume down because
  Higuchi will not turn it up for anyone; Ōgaki phones from Kusunoki SA at the ad break to ask
  why they aren't on it; KANNAKUZU come third and Nukui says something on live television that
  is unmistakably about Komako and that nobody in the country understands except four people.
- **CH31 — "STOOLS."** *MILESTONE — THE PACT.* 2 January, the Hatsuwarai bill, free entry, no
  norma, the eight back-row stools out for the eleventh time in twenty-six years; afterwards they
  stack them, and it is agreed in fifteen words, neither of them looking up from the stools:
  **"After the final." / "Which final?" / "The one we win." / "...After. Not before."**
- **CH32 — "PROFESSIONALS."** The first week inside the pact: everything is identical and nothing
  is; they are more courteous, more precise and more punctual than they have ever been in seven
  years, and Suga watches it from the booth for four nights and finally says eight words about it
  that neither of them can answer.
- **CH33 — "HIGUCH."** *MILESTONE.* March, booth 12, 03:20, the end of a bad month; Higuchi sets
  down two glasses and delivers her first complete sentence of the series — **"Seven years. Never
  once the same side."** — and walks away; and the tag on her apron is a new one, unscratched,
  with the last letter finally on it, which is how two people who have sat in this booth for
  seven years learn that there was a letter missing.

# ARC 4 — THE COST (ch34–44)

**April, year nine, to February, year ten.** Success attacks the duo. Nobody sabotages anybody;
the machine simply wants the two halves at different prices.

**IN-STATE:** a pact with no date on it, a name the industry now says out loud, and a verdict
neither of them can put down.
**OUT-STATE:** the duo chosen at full price, scarred, with a date written on the pact — and one

**LOCKED MILESTONES:** ch38 (ASHITA KARA HONKI dissolves) · ch41 (the solo offer with a number on

- **CH34 — "NO QUOTA."** Theater regular at last: ¥3,000 a set and no norma — and the first week

- **CH35 — "TWELVE SECONDS."** Her first solo panel show: one seat, four hours of recording, four

  nothing, and pays ¥40,000, which is the problem.
- **CH36 — "B-FLAT."** Eleven nights at the key rack with a dated pad and nothing under the date;

  while he is writing it, and hands it over anyway.
- **CH37 — "POLITE."** They perform it and it dies politely — three white gaps, evenly spaced,
  on the beat — and Suga polishes fast circles for the whole four minutes and afterwards says
  eight words that Gaku will still be carrying at chapter 55.
- **CH38 — "SAY THE NAME."** *MILESTONE.* ASHITA KARA HONKI dissolve in the green room after a
  Monday bill; Suehiro makes everybody say the name out loud one last time; Komako is the one who

- **CH39 — "A DRAWER."** Four weeks later Mizobe has a desk, a lanyard, a drawer and eight hours

- **CH40 — "YEAR 9."** 25 July again, **9** in the box; Rounds 1 and 2 compressed into two cold

- **CH41 — "PER EPISODE."** *MILESTONE.* Kujirai brings the solo offer with a figure on it —

- **CH42 — "THE BILL COMES."** The year-nine quarterfinal: they go out, in public, on a stream,

- **CH43 — "TUESDAY'S PAGE."** A weekly runs a 4 a.m. photograph of two people leaving a family

- **CH44 — "OUT OF THE BUILDING."** *MILESTONE.* A television corridor, a producer being
  reasonable, two contracts on a table and only one of them with both names on it; Gaku says the

  from the production company — **and it is not a bit, and nobody laughs, and it is the only time
  in the series the carry happens offstage.** The pact is re-signed that night, over a chore, with
  a date on it: the year-ten final, the last one anybody gets.

# ARC 5 — YEAR TEN (ch45–55)

**March to the third Sunday of December, year ten.** The last eligible year. Everything planted
pays. The elders thaw exactly one rung ahead of the leads.

**IN-STATE:** a duo that survived being wanted separately, a pact with a date, ten months, and a
suit nobody has bought yet.
**OUT-STATE:** the whole story, spent.
**LOCKED MILESTONES:** ch51 (she reads the notebook) · ch52 (he finds the envelope) · ch54 (the
mic, the stairs, the last "Are you two—?") · ch55 (the final, the words, the second eye).

- **CH45 — "THE LAST TWELFTH."** 12 April, worked as always, no acknowledgement of any kind; and
  on the way past Shingei during partner-picking day they stop — properly stop, for four seconds,
  for the first time in eight years — and then walk on and neither of them explains it.
- **CH46 — "THIRTY MINUTES IN FIVE WEEKS."** They burn the act down and rebuild it: thirty
  minutes of new material in five weeks, the hardest work chapter in the series, and Komako writes
  not one word and is indispensable on every single page, and the chapter has to make the reader
  understand exactly why.
- **CH47 — "THE BOX SAYS 10."** 25 July, entry hall full of acts on their last one; a duo two
  rows over write 11 in the box by mistake and are told, quietly, by a member of staff who has
  done this before; Gaku writes **10** and looks at it for a long time before he lets go of the
  pen.
- **CH48 — "OBON."** August, an empty town, a hall like a kiln, Round 1 at 10 a.m. to forty
  people — and Ōgaki drives six hundred kilometres on his rest day to be one of the forty, and
  says nothing about the distance until Komako gets it out of the parking receipt.
- **CH49 — "HE STOOD AT THE BACK."** Rounds 2 and 3 back to back; Gaku's father comes to a live
  for the first time in his life, stands at the back with his coat on, does not laugh, and leaves
  before the house lights come up, and Suga is the only person who sees him go.
- **CH50 — "THE CAPTION."** The quarterfinal, and now they are the story: the broadcast caption
  under their name reads YEAR 10, an entire country does the arithmetic in one second, and for
  the first time in ten years they are on stage in front of people who already know what it costs.
- **CH51 — "OTHER PEOPLE'S LAUGHTER."** *MILESTONE.* She finally reads it — thirty pages of dates
  with one line each, seven years of her own ad-libs, logged by a man who could not say the thing
  so he audited it instead — and she reads it standing up in a corridor, and puts the elastic back
  exactly where it was, and never mentions it to him. Not in this chapter. Not in any chapter.
- **CH52 — "TWO HUNDRED AND ONE."** *MILESTONE.* He finds the rice-cracker tin under the
  passenger seat of the 47 while fetching a strap: ¥201,000, a tally seven years long, TROPHY SUIT
  on the flap in her handwriting, and a department-store tag for ¥189,000 folded inside it. He
  puts the tin back exactly as it was and adds ¥1,000 of his own — **the second person in this
  series to know a thing and hold it.**
- **CH53 — "FOUR NAMES."** The semifinal: twelve in, four out, and the fourth name read is
  theirs; Ōgaki hears it at Kusunoki SA and has to pull over; and in the hall Gaku's father
  exhales once through his nose, which in that family is a standing ovation, and Gaku sees it and
  gets it wrong for eleven seconds and then gets it right.
- **CH54 — "ARE YOU TWO—"** *MILESTONE.* Their last home-stage set: Suga unlocks the flight case
  he has guarded for twenty-six years and hands them GEKKŌ's centre mic without a speech; Shizue
  comes down the arch stairs for the first time in twenty-three years and sits in the back row
  with her coat on and does not speak to him and does not leave; a young act asks the question the
  whole series has been asking — *"Are you two—?"* — and for the first time in fifty-four chapters
  **neither of them says "We're colleagues," and neither of them says anything at all**, and that
  silence is the biggest line in the series.

- **CH55 — "AIBIKI."** *THE FINAL — locked sequence, in this order.*
  Third Sunday of December, live on Chūō Television, 19:00. The caption under their name reads
  **YEAR 10**, and every viewer in the country knows what that means. Ōgaki is in the hall in his
  good jacket, which does not fit him. In the wings, four seconds before they walk, Gaku ties the
  bow tie she has worn untied for ten years — without a word, without being asked, having
  evidently known how the entire time — and she does not ask and does not touch it again.
  **The set IS the confession, performed as material**, and the audience laughs at every true
  line in it, harder each time, because they hear craft; this is the series' knife and it is
  spent here, completely, for the last time. **The four-second hole is left in**, in a broadcast
  hall with no railway within two kilometres — five judges, seven hundred people and an entire
  nation lean into a silence waiting for something that is never coming, and nobody in the
  building knows what it is except two people on stage and one man watching a monitor in a
  sixty-seat basement. **They win** (showrunner's lean; the arc holds either way, but the sequence
  below is written for the win).
  Then: GRAN PORTO, booth 12, 02:40, still in the suits. Two drink bars, ¥299 each. Two aibiki
  hamburgs at **¥790**. Higuchi puts them down and says nothing at all, because one of them is
  sitting on the wrong side of the booth. And in the middle of splitting the bill — mid-chore,
  mid-arithmetic, receipt in hand — **the actual words are said, delivered as a manzai tag**: one
  of them sets it, the other lands it, the way they have ended every set for ten years, and it is
  the only time in the series either of them says it and it takes four bubbles total.
  Then, 04:30, arch 7, house lights on 70%: sixty empty seats, a coat left on the back row, and a
  brush and an ink dish set out beside the daruma on its crate by somebody who had already gone
  home. **FINAL IMAGE: the second eye, painted.**

## PRODUCTION NOTES FOR THE WRITERS' ROOM

- **Every chapter needs a tag.** Write the closing line of the set before you write the chapter.
  If the tag will not come, the set does not work yet. The tag is also, forty minutes later, how
  they say goodnight at Sankaku Bridge — and a chapter that ends on a silent bridge is a chapter
  that ends on a wound. See `series.md` §4.4.
- **Bit-bleed is rationed to once per arc**: ch5, one in arc 2, one in arc 3, one in arc 4, and
  ch55. Log every use in `continuity/log.md`. There is no sixth.
- **The industry beat is capped at one per chapter** and every chapter row above obeys it. "None."
  is the default and should be the answer for roughly half of arc 1.
- **Props that must appear in the background and never be discussed:** the daruma's blank eye,
  the black paint on the jacket cuff from ch6, the untied bow tie, the postcards on the booth
  wall, the flight case, the streaked flyers.
- **Long fuses and where they land:** ch1's word on the envelope flap → ch4 → ch18. Ch2's two
  hoards → ch51/52. Ch6's paint stripe → ch55. Ch9's "then it's a bit, not an exit" → ch55's
  entire method. Ch10's PAY ME BACK IN DECEMBER → ch38/39. Ch11's unsaid tag → ch12. Ch33's
  verdict → ch55's seating.
- **SHOWRUNNER LOCKS (post-review, canon):**
  - **The word on Suga's envelope flap is 「次」— "NEXT."** Credit against your next set: he is
    betting on your future instead of collecting on your night. Visible to the reader from ch1;

  - **KANNAKUZU's clock:** the act formed one year before AIBIKI. Their year ten is AIBIKI's
