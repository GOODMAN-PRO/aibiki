WORLD RENDER RULES (apply on every page):
- STAGE WARDROBE (the act's whole visual identity): ONE charcoal suit worn two ways. GAKU wears it CORRECT: single-breasted, middle button fastened, white shirt, thin solid-black NECKTIE in a tight knot, cuffs buttoned, plain black shoes — jacket and trousers in flat 40% dot screentone, one flat black lapel shadow-shape; he reads as a vertical gray column with one black stripe. KOMAKO wears HIS OLD JACKET a size too big, sleeves rolled to a fat double cuff below the elbow, white shirt with top button open, LONG TROUSERS hemmed short above the ankle (FULL-LENGTH trousers, NEVER shorts, never a skirt) over white socks and black steel-toe work boots — the SAME 40% tone as his, so any two-shot reads as one gray mass with two silhouettes. KOMAKO NEVER WEARS A NECKTIE: around her open collar hangs a black BOW TIE, LOOSE AND UNTIED — draw it as TWO SEPARATE FLAT-BLACK RIBBONS hanging straight down either side of the shirt placket, clearly NOT a necktie. It is never tied (until the scripted chapter says so). Tournament/showcase scenes add a small white hand-numbered paper badge safety-pinned to each left lapel.
- OFFSTAGE / NIGHT DEFAULT WEAR (when the script does not specify): GAKU = gray zip hoodie (20% tone) open over a white tee, flat canvas shoulder bag; KOMAKO = the white stage shirt untucked with the untied bow ribbons still hanging, stage trousers, boots, cargo strap across the body — or Marumo coveralls knotted at the waist over a white tank. NEVER invent generic T-shirts, dresses, or casual outfits not listed here.
- SPOTLIGHT IS NEGATIVE SPACE: never draw a light source, rays, or a gradient glow. On stage, draw the ABSENCE of tone — a hard-edged white pool with the background screentone stopping DEAD at its rim (a crisp tone edge, no soft falloff anywhere); figures inside the pool get no background tone. Stepping out of the light = the tone closes over them.
- THE CROWD: a solid black silhouette bank across the bottom of stage panels, white gaps for teeth and eye-highlights. NO individual faces — except ŌGAKI the trucker, the ONE face in the bank always drawn in full detail. A dying house = the bank with no white gaps at all. When a script names specific audience members (a scout, rivals in row B), draw THOSE figures as flat-ink line art with screentone — the REST of the audience around them stays a black silhouette bank; NEVER render rows of detailed gradient-shaded audience faces. Crowd laughter is hand-lettered mass ("WAHHA"), never inside a bubble; one giggle = tiny "KUSU" alone in white; dead silence = large hollow OUTLINED letters "SHIIN" on a white panel.
- THE SOUND BOOTH: upper rear of house shots — a black rectangle with one white window; the only detail inside is SUGA's square glasses as two flat white ovals, plus a glass and bar towel in his hands.
- THE DARUMA: door-side at hip height on an upturned beer crate — solid black body, flat white face, ONE painted eye (its left / viewer's right), the other a BLANK WHITE CIRCLE facing the room. Beside it a taped cardboard tip box hand-lettered ELECTRICITY. Background of every theater chapter, never remarked.
- THE MIC CASE: battered black flight case under the booth, two chrome latches, cigarette-burn constellation on the lid, one brass padlock. Drawn CLOSED, always (opens only when the script says ch54+).
- VENUE TEXTURE: the arch brick is 25% dot screentone with hand-inked mortar lines on only the two courses nearest the reader — never a fully drawn wall. August: three to five white flick-marks of sweat run down the tone. January: tone drops lighter and breath is drawn as white puffs. One shared center mic on a straight stand at a white gaff-tape X.
- DAY-JOB WEAR: KOMAKO at Marumo Moving = navy coveralls in 30% tone, white oval MARUMO chest patch — the patch is ON THE COVERALLS ONLY, never on the tank top or any other garment; wide black lumbar belt worn high; ON shift = zipped to the throat, OFF shift = top half stripped, sleeves knotted at her waist over a white tank (read her status from the sleeves). GAKU at Capsule Inn Compass = thin flat-black nylon vest over a white shirt, clip badge reading TOKITA / NIGHT always crooked low-right; off duty a gray zip hoodie and a flat canvas shoulder bag on the hip.
- MACHINE TEXT (tabloids, brackets, KPI slides, broadcast captions): hard-geometric all-caps lettering inside a black-bordered box laid at a slight angle, never in a speech bubble, always overlapping some ordinary object in frame.
- SIGNAGE LAW — NEVER INVENT LETTERING. Any poster, flyer, banner, sign or shopfront in a panel may ONLY carry text the PAGE SCRIPT explicitly supplies (in its captions or panel description). If the script gives no wording, draw the object with its lettering illegible — blurred strokes, a torn corner, an oblique angle — never a name you made up. In particular: MARUMO is a MOVING COMPANY and its name appears ONLY on navy coveralls and a two-ton truck — never on a poster, flyer, venue, or event of any kind. The tournament is always FUTTONDA-1 GRAND PRIX; the theater is always GEKIJŌ GAKEPPUCHI.
